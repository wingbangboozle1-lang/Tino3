Deadlines and key dates

Every legal and tax clock in order, from death and from transfer, with its official source.

First gate: is the transfer between a parent and a child?

Every reassessment figure on this site assumes the transfer will qualify for the intergenerational exclusion in Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2. That section excludes a transfer only where it is between parents and their children, or between grandparents and grandchildren where every parent of the grandchild who is a child of the grandparent has died. “Children” is defined in the statute to include a stepchild, a son-in-law or daughter-in-law, and an adopted child adopted before age 18; it also includes a foster child of a licensed foster parent where adoption was barred. The relationship is a condition of the exclusion, not a detail: if the transferor and the heir are not in one of those relationships, no exclusion is available, the property is reassessed to full market value, and the entire saving shown on this page disappears.

This analysis is written on the assumption that it is. The trust document is the only thing that confirms it, and this site has not seen it. Until it is confirmed, treat every Proposition 19 shelter figure here as conditional. See question A-1.

The next dated item

The first installment of the 2026/27 secured bill must be paid, or postmarked, by 10 December 2026 to avoid penalties. That payment is an obligation only while Property A is still held. Two other official dates also fall in the next year, and both are listed in the table below: 11 December 2026, the statewide certification that the law-watch figures depend on, and 16 February 2027, the next Proposition 19 adjustment. The February date sets the shelter amount for transfers from that day; the 2040s figures on this site use a projected growth rate, because no later amount is published yet. Everything else is measured from the date of death or the date of transfer.

Which deadlines can move the answer

The three deadlines that can change the recommendation are: (1) whether the parent-child relationship is in place before the transfer, because without it the Proposition 19 shelter disappears; (2) the one-year homeowners' exemption and three-year exclusion-claim windows, because missing them costs the shelter; and (3) the nine-month estate-tax return, because portability is normally lost when no timely return is filed - although an estate not otherwise required to file can use the simplified late election of Rev. Proc. 2022-32 within five years of death. The estate page works entirely in anonymous scenario bands because the inherited cash amount is not published.

The decision calendar, before the death

Most of these tasks have no statute behind them, and that is the point

Everything above is a legal clock that starts at a death. The clocks below start now, and they matter more, because each one changes a number the decision depends on. None is triggered by a statute; each is triggered by someone deciding to ask. They are listed with the latest sensible date and what the answer changes.

The dates are planning advice, not law, so they carry no official source. That is the honest status: a timeline of your own actions cannot be "verified" against an official page, only against your own calendar.

The written investing policy, before the money arrives

Write it down before the sale, not after

The recommendation on this site is to sell and invest. That creates a second decision the brief did not ask for but cannot avoid: what the proceeds are invested in, and by what rule. A policy written after the sale is a description of whatever was bought, not a constraint on it. The six lines below are the questions the policy has to answer, and they are deliberately left blank.

The phased plan on the projections page parks the proceeds in short Treasuries for 24 to 36 months and phases into equities over the following 12. That plan is a schedule. It is not a policy, and it does not answer any of the lines below.

Why the order matters

  1. The trust notice (60 days) and the change-in-ownership statement (150 days) are the first two clocks after death.
  2. The federal return, if one is needed, comes at nine months, and Form 4768 is the only automatic way to extend it. An extension of time to file is not an extension of time to pay: see the estate liquidity page.
  3. The exclusion claim has a three-year window, but the occupancy and homeowners' exemption steps are one year. Missing the one-year steps is the most expensive mistake that can be made on the timeline.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed.
