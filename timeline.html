Deadlines as a timeline

Deadlines that start at death or at transfer, and fixed dates in the current law.

How to read this timeline

The inheritance is not one event. Some clocks start at death, some at the transfer, and some are fixed by the calendar. Each row names its source. Dates the site cannot confirm are not filled in.

In order

  1. Date of death (month 0). Locate the trust document and confirm the transfer relationship.
  2. Months 0 to 9. Decide whether a federal return is needed (threshold), and file it or extend it on Form 4768.
  3. Months 0 to 12. Decide whether the house will be lived in. If so, the homeowners' exemption and principal-residence start must happen within one year of transfer.
  4. Years 1 to 3. File the intergenerational transfer claim before the three-year limit.
  5. Years 3 to 5. Last point for a late portability election if no filing was required earlier.

The order matters more than the exact months: the homeowners' exemption and the claim are the two steps that lose the benefit if missed.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
