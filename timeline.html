Deadlines and key dates

Every legal and tax clock in order, from death and from transfer, with its official source.

First gate: is the transfer between a parent and a child?

Every reassessment figure on this site assumes the transfer will qualify for the intergenerational exclusion in Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2. That section excludes a transfer only where it is between parents and their children, or between grandparents and grandchildren where every parent of the grandchild who is a child of the grandparent has died. “Children” is defined in the statute to include a stepchild, a son-in-law or daughter-in-law, and an adopted child adopted before age 18; it also includes a foster child of a licensed foster parent where adoption was barred. The relationship is a condition of the exclusion, not a detail: if the transferor and the heir are not in one of those relationships, no exclusion is available, the property is reassessed to full market value, and the entire saving shown on this page disappears.

This analysis is written on the assumption that it is. The trust document is the only thing that confirms it, and this site has not seen it. Until it is confirmed, treat every Proposition 19 shelter figure here as conditional. See question A-1.

Which deadlines can move the answer

The three deadlines that can change the recommendation are: (1) whether the parent-child relationship is in place before the transfer, because without it the Proposition 19 shelter disappears; (2) the one-year homeowners' exemption and three-year exclusion-claim windows, because missing them costs the shelter; and (3) the nine-month estate-tax return, because a late return loses the portability election. The estate page works entirely in anonymous scenario bands because the inherited cash amount is not published.

Why the order matters

  1. The trust notice (60 days) and the change-in-ownership statement (150 days) are the first two clocks after death.
  2. The federal return, if one is needed, comes at nine months, and Form 4768 is the only automatic way to extend it.
  3. The exclusion claim has a three-year window, but the occupancy and homeowners' exemption steps are one year. Missing the one-year steps is the most expensive mistake that can be made on the timeline.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed.
