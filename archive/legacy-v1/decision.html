Why selling still gets the recommendation

  1. Risk, not just return. A single house in one county is one asset with one buyer pool. The comparison above gives equities a 7% return; it does not price the concentration risk of the alternative.
  2. The tax cost of waiting. After the step-up, every dollar of appreciation is taxable at 32.3% when eventually sold. Holding converts a tax-free gain into a taxed one.
  3. Liquidity. A sale converts an illiquid asset into a portfolio that can fund the household's housing, care and expenses without a forced sale at a bad moment.
  4. Optionality. The household keeps a place to live in B. Nothing about selling A removes the option to buy elsewhere later.

The counter-case, stated fairly: if appreciation runs at 5% and portfolio returns at 4–5%, renting A is worth $2,754,760 more by 2055. Landlording a single-family home also produces no capital-gains tax while held, and the pending proposal would widen that gap. Anyone who believes Bay Area land compounds above 5% should rent, not sell.
