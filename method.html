How the comparison works

  1. Values, assessed values and costs are projected year by year from 2026.
  2. In the year of inheritance each option is started: live in Property A, rent it out, or sell it and invest.
  3. For 20 years every option's yearly cash, after tax, is added to a savings pot that earns the after-tax Treasury rate. Shortfalls are taken from the same pot.
  4. At the end the house or portfolio is valued as if sold, after selling costs and tax, and added to the pot.
  5. Totals are also shown in today's dollars by removing 2.4% a year of inflation.

The model leaves out mortgage debt, the heir's other income, earthquake risk, major one-off repairs, and the difference in comfort between the two homes. It treats every option as taxed on sale at the end, which understates any asset the heir would hold for life.

How personal data is kept out

  • No names, addresses, parcel numbers, postal codes, neighbourhoods or account numbers appear, and no parcel was looked up.
  • Assessed values and tax bills are public records. A figure rounded to $10 can still be matched to one parcel, so those series are shown as rounded levels: about $550,000 and $120,000 of assessed value, about $7,200 and $2,400 of tax. Every projection is calculated from the rounded levels.
  • The effect is small. Re-running the model on the owner's exact figures changes no yearly figure by more than $100 and no 20-year result by more than $1,000, and the ranking is the same.
  • Every amount is a multiple of $10.

Reproducing the figures

The model, its inputs and the page generator are in the plan folder of the repository. python plan/model/build_site.py rebuilds every page; python scripts/verify.py checks sources, links, rounding and the privacy rules. An owner can run the model on exact figures offline by putting them in the git-ignored private folder.
