Attorney & CPA briefing sheet

A one-page printable briefing sheet for the heir's attorney and CPA.

Privacy and accuracy notice

  • What is published. Market values, assessed values, tax bills, rent and running costs, rounded to the nearest $10 as requested, plus the model outputs computed from them.
  • What is never published. No name, street address, parcel number, ZIP code, trust term, beneficiary detail or utility account appears on any page or in any published file. The two properties are called Property A (the home being inherited, in Santa Clara County) and Property B (the three-unit building already owned by the heir, in the City and County of San Francisco).
  • The inherited cash. The amount is not published. The estate page works entirely in anonymous scenario bands.
  • Rounding is not anonymity. Rounding to $10 defeats a search for an exact figure; it does not stop someone who already holds the county record from recognising a parcel. That residual risk is disclosed here rather than hidden.
  • Owner figures are unverified. Values taken from the owner's own records are labelled unverified unless an official public record was checked. They are listed again on the flagged for review page.
  • History. Earlier commits in this public repository, previous pull-request diffs, forks and external copies are separate exposure surfaces. Nothing on this site can certify them erased.

What would change the answer

In no particular order.

  • The transfer relationship. The Proposition 19 exclusion applies only to a parent-child transfer (or grandparent-grandchild where the middle generation has died). If it does not apply, every reassessment figure here falls away and the case for selling gets stronger. The trust document decides it.
  • The heir's plans. Living in the home keeps the tax bill lower and is worth the whole gap if it is true for the whole horizon; moving out removes the exclusion.
  • Market rent. Letting works only if the market rent clears about $34,260 a month in 2045. No market rent has been supplied for Property A.
  • House price growth. Keeping the home beats selling only if it appreciates about 7.72% a year for thirty years.
  • Tax law changes. Federal capital-gains and net investment income tax rates, the California top rate, the scope of Proposition 19 and the federal estate-tax exclusion would each move the terminal values.
  • Estate size. The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 in 2026 and $15,480,000 in 2027; an estate above it pays federal estate tax on the excess.

For the attorney

  • Confirm whether the transfer qualifies for the Proposition 19 parent-child exclusion (RTC 63.2) before any reassessment figure is relied on; read the trust document.
  • Whether a 1031 exchange, an instalment sale, or trust restructuring should be considered - none of these are modelled here.
  • Whether the estate is large enough for federal estate tax to matter, and whether portability planning is in place.

For the CPA

  • Test the break-even rent of about $34,260 a month in 2045 against an actual market rent opinion before relying on any letting path.
  • The phased-investment path gives up $2,016,490 of baseline upside to remove the risk of investing the whole lump sum at a market peak.
  • Depreciation recapture and California tax apply if the home is let out and later sold.
  • No market rent, insurance quote, or utility tariff for Property A has been verified; all are flagged on the review page.
