What to change, and in what order

Four paths, 23 suggestions, and the privacy trade-off, so a route can be chosen before anything is implemented.

How this page works. These are suggestions, not changes. Each one states what it fixes, what it costs in time, and what it does to privacy. Nothing on the site changes unless you choose it, apart from the two items marked Done in this edition, which are described at the foot of the page. Read the paths first, pick one, then work down the list.

Choose a path before changing anything

There are four sensible paths. They differ in how much is settled before the decision is made, not in what is recommended: every path ends in the same default, sell Property A and keep Property B, because the ranking does not depend on the missing numbers. What the missing numbers change is the size of the margin and the tax bill.

The privacy choice

The brief asks for property tax and assessment tables at $10 precision, so that figures cannot be traced back to the source. That is the setting in force now. It is worth being exact about what it does: rounding to $10 removes the exact match, and nothing more. A roll value within a few dollars of the record, published with a 2% growth chain, is a strong identifier to anyone who can search the county roll. The stronger protection is rounding to $1,000 or $10,000, which costs the last digits of a table but none of the analysis.

Decision gates

Gate 1 · now, no spending

Read the trust and the deed; confirm which home carries the homeowners' exemption; decide whether anyone would move in; put the documents in one folder. All of it is free, and it closes the two questions that block the analysis.

Gate 2 · before 2035

Professional review of the trust, the portability election and any inherited accounts; three insurance quotes at that year's prices, renewed every three years; a baseline appraisal so the eventual date-of-death value has a reference point.

Gate 3 · the twelve months after the death

Appraisal, change in ownership statement within 150 days, homeowners' exemption within one year, exclusion claim within three years or before a sale, Form 706 within nine months if the estate is near the threshold or a spouse survives. Then the decision, with real numbers instead of scenarios.

Gate 4 · every year

Re-read three law-watch items, update the Treasury yield, re-run the model, and re-read the break-even table. If any of the four break-evens is crossed, the ranking changes and this page should be read again.
