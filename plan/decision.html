Decision analysis

Live in Property A, rent it out, or sell it and reinvest: the first year in dollars, the ten-year outcome, and the break-even points. Property B is kept in every option.

Recommended plan

  1. Now, while the owner is alive: change nothing about title. Property passing at death gets a new income-tax basis equal to its value at the date of death, so the gain built up over the owner's lifetime is never taxed. The houses alone are $10,100,000 under the 2026 federal exclusion; California has no estate or inheritance tax.
  2. Keep Property B. Net rent is 4.27% of its value today, or 3.16% if it is reassessed at inheritance. Even then, net rent plus the lowest house-price growth tested (5.26% together) is above the 4.88% 10-year Treasury yield, and the third unit is the heir's home.
  3. Property A, default: sell within the first year after inheritance and reinvest. In that window the sale is almost free of capital-gains tax, and it removes a property-tax bill that jumps from $10,700 to between $48,600 and $74,110 a year (2045, base case). Before income tax, selling beats living in the house by $908,570 over ten years.
  4. Switch to “keep and rent out” only if a written rent estimate at that time is above about 2.41% of the house's value per year ($8,630 a month in today's dollars for 2045). No verified market rent exists today, so this cell is deliberately blank.
  5. Switch to “live in it” only if you expect the house to appreciate faster than about 4.90% a year, or your tax rate on interest income is above about 25% and the sale money would sit in taxable bonds. Then move in and file the Proposition 19 claim and homeowners' exemption within one year.
  6. If still undecided nine months after the death, move in and file. The exclusion cannot be claimed later, while the option to sell stays open.

The call is close: selling wins 9 of the 18 cases tested (three inheritance years, three appreciation rates, before and after tax). The numbers are on the decision page; everything that could not be verified is on the Flagged for Review page.
