Estate and inheritance tax thresholds

Federal thresholds, California's position, scenario bands.

The short answer

California has no estate tax and no inheritance tax. The State Controller's Office confirms that no California estate tax return is required for a death on or after 1 January 2005, and the Franchise Tax Board confirms that a gift or inheritance is not California income.

Federally, the exclusion for 2026 is $15,000,000 per person and $15,480,000 for 2027. A surviving spouse can elect to use a deceased spouse's unused exclusion, so a couple can shelter $30,000,000 between them if that election is made on a timely return.

What this page cannot tell you

  • Whether the estate exceeds the exclusion. The amount of the cash inheritance is deliberately not published here and not modelled as a specific figure. Use the scenario table and compare it with the estate's own numbers privately.
  • Whether prior taxable gifts have already used part of the exclusion. Lifetime taxable gifts are added back in the computation.
  • Whether any of the estate is a retirement account. Those are taxed differently and are the most common surprise.
  • What the law will be in 2040 to 2050. The exclusion has moved in both directions historically; it was $5,490,000 in 2017 and $15,000,000 in 2026. See the law page.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
