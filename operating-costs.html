Water

San Francisco water rates rose 7.0% on 1 July 2026 and are scheduled to rise another 7.0% on 1 July 2027; sewer rates rose 15.0% and are scheduled to rise 14.5%. The SFPUC puts the average single-family combined water-and-sewer bill at $189 a month in fiscal 2027 and $212 in fiscal 2028 SFPUC rates↗.

Property B's owner-supplied range of $330 to $400 a month for three units is consistent with three households sharing a bill of that order. Property A's $100 to $150 a month is plausible for a single house with irrigation, but Cupertino is not an SFPUC customer, so that range is carried as supplied and escalated at 6.0% — between the water and sewer steps (F-08).

Electricity and the solar credit

Property A generates a small net credit today. The confidence of that credit depends on which net-metering tariff the array is on. The CPUC has closed the original net energy metering tariffs to new enrolments: systems applying after 15 April 2023 take the net billing tariff under decision D.22-12-056, which pays for exported power at a value-of-generation rate rather than the retail rate CPUC NEM/NBT↗.

Two consequences the projection does not capture. First, a legacy net-metering term runs for a fixed period from interconnection, so a system installed in the 2010s or 2020s may reach the end of its term around the inheritance window — at which point the credit becomes an export payment and the annual electricity cost probably turns positive. Second, the CPUC's tariffs are not fixed for 30 years; they are relitigated. The model keeps the owner's net credit and inflates it, which is the optimistic case ASSUMPTION. The solar system's tariff vintage and term end date are item F-05.

Repairs and maintenance

Both figures are assumptions proportional to market value: 0.50% a year for the single-family house and 1.00% for the older three-unit building, which matches the owner's own observation that the Cupertino house needs less work. On today's values that is $15,500 and $18,000 a year. A roof, a sewer lateral or a foundation repair arrives as a lump, not as a percentage; treat these as a sinking fund and hold a reserve.
