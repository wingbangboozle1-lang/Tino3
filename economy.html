The verified historical record

Fifty years of verified inflation, house price and yield data.

Why this page exists

The black swan page uses this record rather than invented scenarios. Every number below comes from one of four series hosted by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis on behalf of the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Federal Housing Finance Agency and the Board of Governors. Where a series stops before 2025, the table shows a dash rather than filling the gap.

The tables after the annual record answer the objection that a single constant rate is unrealistic, in the one way the record supports: the fifty years to 2026 are sliced into ten-year blocks and redrawn two thousand times, so the projections become distributions instead of one guess. The last tables show what the record's inflation does to the rules that are written in fixed nominal dollars.

Two thousand fifty-year economies, redrawn from the record

Ten-year blocks of the official annual record are drawn with replacement - 39 blocks where both the house price series and the consumer price series are present - into 2,000 fifty-year paths. House and inflation history move together inside a block, as they did in the record. The percentile of each outcome is shown against the constant-model case.

A pure asset-price race, and nothing more: the sell path deducts selling costs and then earns the net equity return; the hold path earns only the house price path. How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
