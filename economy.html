Black swans

Growth and inflation are never smooth

Fifty years of official annual data, decade by decade, and a fifty-year range drawn from real ten-year stretches instead of one flat rate.

Why this page exists. Every other projection on this site holds one rate flat for decades: house prices at 4%, costs at 2.5%. Nobody who has lived through the last fifty years believes that is what happens. This page puts the actual year-by-year record beside those flat rates, and then builds a fifty-year range by re-using real ten-year stretches of it rather than inventing a random walk.

Fifty years, drawn from real decades

Instead of compounding one rate for fifty years, this model takes the record and cuts it into whole ten-year stretches. It then draws 2,000 fifty-year paths by picking five stretches at random and applying them in order to Property A's current value. Each stretch carries both its inflation and its house-price behaviour together, because they happened together. This is not a forecast and not a simulation of the future; it is a way of asking how wide the range of past outcomes was for an asset held for fifty years.

What this changes, and what it does not. It does not change the recommendation: the decision analysis is made over ten years after an inheritance, and it already holds in the stress cases. What it changes is the confidence with which anyone should say "the house will be worth about X in 2076". The honest answer is a range of roughly $8,118,410 to $39,045,920, not one number — and the lower end is a real outcome that has happened, not a pessimistic flourish.

How to use a range in the decision

  1. Ask what the decision needs to be right about. Selling and reinvesting does not depend on the 2076 house price at all; it converts the house into a diversified asset at the appraisal value. Renting and living in depend on it completely.
  2. Treat the median as a midpoint, not a target. Half the resampled paths end below it.
  3. Check the downside against your own liquidity. A decade like 2007–2016 is survivable with cash and a job and ruinous with neither, whatever the fifty-year average says.
  4. Re-run it when the record changes. The script reads the published series, so next year's data produces next year's range.

What is not modelled here

  • No taxes, costs or rent. This is the asset's price history only. The decision model, with its property tax, insurance, maintenance and sale costs, is on the decision page.
  • No correlation with your other assets. Property B is in the same state; the record above is the same record for both, so the two are not independent.
  • No future law. The 2% assessment cap, the exclusion and the tax rates are held at today's rules.
  • No claim that the past bounds the future. A path outside every one drawn is entirely possible; it simply has no precedent in this record.
