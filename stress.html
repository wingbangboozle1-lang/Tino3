Black swan stress tests

What happens when the economy stops cooperating

Verified 1929, 1970s, 2000 and 2008 episodes re-run against the 2045 inheritance; the amounts that do not rise with inflation; and what changes and what does not.

Black swans, part 2 of 2 — stress tests. The verified episodes and the inheritance decision re-run under each of them. Part 1 sets out the baseline and the fifty-year record it contradicts: conditions and history.

Selling Property A and reinvesting wins in 5 of the 7 historical analogues; renting wins only when equities flatline for a decade. Moving in never wins. The reinvestment vehicle, however, depends on the regime.

Over 2040-2060 the economy will not move in a straight line. The official record contains a depression, two inflationary oil shocks, a dot-com collapse, a housing crash, a pandemic crash and a 9.1% inflation print. This page re-runs the 2045 inheritance decision under the shapes of seven real episodes. The sale decision itself is robust; the vehicle and the margin are not.

Why this section exists

The inheritance is expected between 2040 and 2050, and the projections on this site run to 2060 - a horizon of 10 to 35 years, and a working lifetime of 50. Over that length, growth and inflation are never consistent: the base case here (2.5% inflation, 4% house growth, 7% stock return) is a central scenario, not a forecast. Every figure below that is not from an official publisher is computed scenario arithmetic from that grid, and is labelled as such.

Stress tests applied to the 2045 inheritance

Method. The market value at the 2045 inheritance, the tax basis reset, the Proposition 19 rule, the 2% assessed-value cap, the 10-year comparison window, and every tax rate are identical to the assumptions on the decision page. Only the post-inheritance economy is swapped: inflation (which drives costs), house price growth after 2045, stock returns, and the 10-year Treasury yield. Every option is sold and taxed at the end of the window, so the options remain comparable. House and stock cells marked path apply the year-by-year sequence of the historical analogue (crash first, recovery after); the 2045 market value itself is anchored to the baseline path. The federal estate exclusion IS indexed (chained CPI), so its real value is broadly preserved, subject to Congress: see the scenario table on the tax page. The Proposition 19 amount is indexed too, but only by one year of house price change every second year, so it grows more slowly than house prices and erodes slowly. The IRC 121 exclusion is not indexed at all: at 8% inflation the joint exclusion is worth under $120,000 in 2045 and under $80,000 in 2050, against $500,000 today.

What changes - and what does not

  1. The decision to sell survives. Selling and reinvesting wins or nearly ties in all seven analogues; moving into the home is last in every one of them. The sale also monetises the tax-basis reset, which no later regime can reverse.
  2. The vehicle depends on the regime. Treasuries win when yields are high enough to clear inflation (S1 at 8.0%, S5 at 8.5%) and when stocks crash at the inheritance (S4); equities win in baseline and boom conditions (S0, S6); renting wins only in flat-equity analogues (S2 by $279,350, S3 by $330,540 - both under 5% of the comparison base).
  3. Treasuries are not a hedge against high inflation at today's yield. At 4.88% with 6-9% inflation, each dollar in Treasuries loses 1-4% of real value a year. The S1 and S5 results assume the Treasury yield rises with inflation, as it did in the 1970s-80s; at today's 4.88% held constant, renting and equities win S1 clearly.
  4. Timing the sale matters less than not being forced at the bottom. S4 shows a −45% crash landing on the inheritance: the stock leg ends ten years later at only $6,833,840, behind Treasuries at $7,152,850, and renting nearly ties selling. An inheritance landing mid-crash is the one case for holding the home 1-2 years and selling into the recovery.
  5. Nominal rents lag inflation by construction. Sitting-tenant increases are 60% of the regional index (1.5% at the assumed 2.5% inflation), so real rent declines every year in nominal-terms; that is the structural weakness of the rent option in an inflation regime.
  6. Concentration is itself a black swan amplifier. Both properties are in one region; a local shock hits value, rent demand and insurance availability at once (insurance has repriced nationally after the 2023-2025 wildfire and storm losses - no quote is verified here; the flagged page lists premium uncertainty). Selling converts one regional, single-asset exposure into a diversified portfolio.

Fifty years, three regimes

1965-1982 rewarded rent and real assets; 1983-2000 rewarded houses and stocks together; 2000-2009 punished stocks and then housing; 2020-2022 punished everything except nominal cash yield. A plan that is optimal in exactly one of those regimes is not robust. The diversified answer the numbers keep favouring: sell A at the inheritance (the basis reset is certain; everything else is contingent), reinvest in a mix weighted to equities with a Treasury ballast sized to the then-current yield, keep Property B for its rent, and revisit the split every two years as the regime reveals itself.

Black-swan playbook for the inheritance window

  1. Get the date-of-death appraisal first. Every downstream figure - basis reset, Proposition 19 amount, federal estate calculation - keys off it.
  2. Do not be a forced seller into a crash. The first 12-24 months are the riskiest time to sell a home into an air pocket. If the 2040-2050 economy prints a black swan, the fallback is to rent A temporarily (the model prices this) rather than sell at the trough.
  3. File the exclusion claims on time. One year to occupy, three years to claim (or before a sale). A missed deadline is a pure loss of roughly $18,250 a year.
  4. Elect portability on Form 706 if a spouse survives, even with no tax due - the second exclusion is forfeited otherwise, and a black swan later can not be unwound.
  5. Stress the insurance, not the premium guess. Rebuild costs have repriced; get quotes at renewal and after any remodel; a lapse or underinsured total loss is the one unrecoverable black swan for the house itself.
  6. Keep 1-2 years of Property A running costs liquid at the inheritance so a forced sale is never required.
  7. Re-run this page's arithmetic every two years - the scenario grid, the Treasury yield and the tax thresholds all move; the conclusions should be re-earned, not inherited.
  8. Keep the fixed-rate bias. No debt is modeled in this plan; if any is ever added to A or B, fix the rate - 1981 showed what floating does to a stretched landlord.
  9. Two-year residency before any later sale of A if the family moves in, to keep the (unindexed) $500,000 joint home-sale exclusion available - worth about $197,870 in 2045 at 2.5% inflation, far less in a high-inflation regime.

What this page is not

Not a forecast, and not a timing tool. S4's year-by-year path is constructed from the verified magnitudes of the 2008 analogue, not from any prediction that 2045 will repeat it. The scenarios are deliberately wide on both sides. What the analysis supports is qualitative but firm: the sale at the inheritance is robust to every regime in the official record; the destination of the proceeds is regime-dependent and should be set with the yields and prices then in front of you.
