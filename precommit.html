Decide before you read the numbers

The household's rules, written before the figures are read.

Read this page before any other page

This page is the household's own rulebook, and it is meant to be read before the projections. The owner's instruction was explicit: agree in writing what would make the household choose each path before the figures arrive, so the outcome cannot be reverse-engineered from whichever projection is shown first. Read the rules below, fill in the two decision columns when the household decides, and only then read the numbers.

Nothing here has been decided. The register is a testing rule, not a recommendation, and the recommendation on the decision page is what the model says after the test, not instead of it.

A financial model cannot rank these. A stated preference to keep a home in the family is legitimate and can outweigh a six-figure difference; the point of writing it down first is that the figures are then tested against the preference instead of the other way round.

Why the rules come before the numbers

A single projection can be made to support almost any path, because it depends on a handful of assumptions that nobody can verify: house prices over thirty years, the return on the reinvested money, market rent, and the cost of holding the building. Writing the rule down first changes the question from “which path does this projection favour?” to “does the evidence clear the bar the household set in advance?”. That is the only defence against choosing a path and then finding the assumptions that justify it.

How the test is applied

  1. Clear the first gate. Answer question A-1 on the questions page: is the transfer between a parent and a child? If it is not, the Proposition 19 exclusion does not exist and the rules below are re-read with a full reassessment.
  2. State the goals in the order they actually matter to the household, not in the order the model ranks them.
  3. Set the rules - the conditions under which each path is the chosen one.
  4. Only then read the projections, and ask whether they clear the rules, not whether they look favourable.
  5. Record the decision and its date beside the rule, and re-read the rules every year at gate RG-3.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
