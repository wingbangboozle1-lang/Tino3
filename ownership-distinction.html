The distinction that changes the arithmetic

An inherited property arrives with a reset income-tax basis and a property-tax assessment about to reset the other way. An already-owned property has neither event. Treating the two as interchangeable is the single easiest way to reach the wrong answer here, because the reset basis makes selling cheap at exactly the moment the reassessment makes holding expensive.

One exemption, two properties

California allows one homeowners' exemption. If the heir claims the Proposition 19 exclusion on Property A, Property A must be the principal residence, and the Property B position has to be given up. The exclusion also requires continued occupation: it is removed on the date the home stops qualifying, whether because the heir moves out, lets it, or simply stops using it as a main home. There are no exceptions to the one-year occupation rule. That makes "move in for a while, then let it out" a risky structure, not a safe compromise.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
