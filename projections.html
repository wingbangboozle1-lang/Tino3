Why there is a band and a line

The tables below are one line: 4.0% a year, every year. The chart above is the same property value drawn from the seeded block-bootstrap on the historical record page, which resamples the actual ten-year runs of California house prices and consumer prices instead of assuming they repeat. The band is wide, and the single line sits inside it rather than at its middle. Neither is a forecast; the chart's purpose is to stop a single line being read as one.

The band is a 5th-to-95th percentile interval, so one path in ten ends outside it on each side. The distribution is skewed: the middle path is well above the constant line at the far end, because the bootstrap includes the high-growth decades of the record, while the low end is limited by how far a house price can fall.

Year by year, with the record's own variation

The tables on this page use one constant growth rate so the arithmetic is legible. That is not what the record looks like: annual house price growth in the California record has run from minus 20.0% to plus 25.3%. The fan below redraws both paths using 2000 resampled fifty-year histories of the verified series, drawn in ten-year blocks so house prices and consumer prices move together as they did. The method is the economy page's.

After thirty years, by decile of the verified record

The constant 4% a year gives $6,531,230 at the 2045 inheritance. The 2000 resampled histories put the value at the inheritance anywhere from $2,188,990 to $30,686,600, and the thirty-year outcome for either path across a much wider band.

Read the tails, not the middle. In the bottom decile the household that lived in the house is close to the household that sold, because house prices there did badly while the sale proceeds still earned the modelled equity return. In the top decile keeping the house wins by more than a hundred million nominal dollars. The median favours selling in every comparison, and the spread is why the answer is published as a share of draws.

Read the two threshold tables together

The federal exclusion is indexed to inflation and has been rising. The Proposition 19 shelter is adjusted only every second February and only by the change in the California house price index. If house prices rise faster than the shelter, the shelter buys a smaller and smaller share of the property each time the transfer is delayed. On the baseline assumptions the shelter covers about 29% of Property A's modelled 2045 value. Waiting is not neutral.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
