Projection method — no personal forecast

A reproducible method for private scenario comparisons without publishing owner inputs, property-specific projections or a smooth 50-year growth forecast.

No personalized projection is published. The public site contains no starting property value, rent, assessed value, owner-supplied input or calculated future outcome. A 50-year line based on a single growth rate would imply more certainty than the evidence supports.

Build privately, in separate ledgers

  1. Start with verified title, current bills, rent records, basis/depreciation records, insurance and written repair estimates.
  2. Keep Property A's inheritance, basis, reassessment eligibility and use options separate from Property B's existing ownership and rental cash flow.
  3. Use annual time steps for cash receipts, expenses, tax rules and occupancy changes. Distinguish nominal dollars from inflation-adjusted purchasing power.
  4. Use multiple paths, not a constant growth rate: declines, rebounds, flat real prices, changing inflation, changing rates, vacancy, insurance repricing and major repair years.
  5. Show the assumptions and source for each input; leave unsupported inputs blank. Do not turn a scenario into a probability or recommendation without evidence.

Use horizons, not false precision

For each chosen horizon, compare (1) after-tax net cash flow, (2) net sale proceeds or remaining asset value, (3) investment alternatives, (4) housing value and any rent foregone by occupying B, and (5) liquidity and management risk. A historical episode is a stress example, not a prediction. The Economic stress cases page documents the episodes used as reminders.
