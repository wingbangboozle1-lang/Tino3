What the cases do not show

No case here credits the letting paths with a market rent, because none has been supplied. Read every letting row as the path at zero rent, which is a floor. Lever 3 credits the living path instead, at stated levels that are scenarios rather than estimates; the input that would hold a real imputed rent is empty, so the published baseline stays the zero row. Levers 4 and 5 change a cost rather than a return, and they are there to show what does not move the answer: neither the insurance band nor any debt service tested here puts keeping the house ahead of selling it. Lever 6 runs the borrowing case end to end for the same reason: at the published rate no loan-to-value puts the borrow-and-keep path ahead of selling, and the two ways it clearly loses - parking the loan in Treasuries, and meeting a crash at entry - are rows of their own. The model also does not change the Proposition 19 eligibility question; if that answer is no, the reassessment is full and every case here moves toward selling.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
