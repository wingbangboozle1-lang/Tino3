Taxes & estate

Three governments, three different questions. California asks nothing when you inherit. Washington asks whether the estate is large enough to file. Your own income tax starts only when the asset earns or is sold.

The inheritance amount you mentioned

There is no California threshold to cross: no state inheritance or estate tax exists, so no amount of inherited money triggers a state penalty. The only threshold that matters is the federal one, and it applies to the whole estate before it is divided, not to what any one person receives. The two houses are worth about $4,900,000 today, which leaves $10,100,000 of headroom in 2026. Cash, brokerage and retirement accounts have to be added to that before the question is answered — and that figure is not in this project (F-01).

What this model does not tax

Four omissions are deliberate, because including them would require facts that are not available: passive-activity loss limits on rental real estate; state and federal alternative minimum taxes; the interaction of a large one-off gain with the rest of your income in that year; and any estate or income tax on retirement accounts. Each of these can move a year's bill materially. They are listed on the flagged page rather than quietly assumed away.
