Glossary of terms

Plain-language definitions of the terms used on this site.

Privacy and accuracy notice

  • What is published. Market values, assessed values, tax bills, rent and running costs, rounded to the nearest $10 as requested, plus the model outputs computed from them.
  • What is never published. No name, street address, parcel number, ZIP code, county or city name, trust term, beneficiary detail or utility account appears on any page or in any published file. The two properties are called Property A (the home being inherited) and Property B (the three-unit building already owned by the heir). Their county and city are deliberately withheld: like the parcel, they identify the properties, so official county and city pages are not linked from the public site either.
  • The inherited cash. The amount is not published. The estate page works entirely in anonymous scenario bands.
  • Rounding is not anonymity. Rounding to $10 defeats a search for an exact figure; it does not stop someone who already holds the county record from recognising a parcel. That residual risk is disclosed here rather than hidden.
  • Owner figures are unverified. Values taken from the owner's own records are labelled unverified unless an official public record was checked. They are listed again on the flagged for review page.
  • History. Earlier commits in this public repository, previous pull-request diffs, forks and external copies are separate exposure surfaces. Nothing on this site can certify them erased.

Like-kind exchange (section 1031)A swap of real property held for use in a trade or business or for investment for other real property of like kind, on which no gain is recognised. It defers the tax rather than forgiving it: the replacement takes the old basis, so the deferred gain is still there when the replacement is eventually sold. The replacement must be identified in writing within 45 days of the transfer and received within 180 days, or by the due date of the return including extensions if that is earlier.U.S. Government Publishing Office

Phased investingSelling the inherited home, holding the proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasury bills for 24-36 months, then buying equities in equal monthly pieces over the following 12 months. EncumbranceA mortgage, deed of trust, tax lien, assessment lien or other claim registered against a property. Nothing in the record says whether either building here carries one, so the model runs both free and clear and the decision page states that as an assumption rather than a fact.plain-language term; not from an official source Logged on the register
Due-on-sale clauseA loan term letting a lender demand full repayment when the property is transferred. Federal law stops a lender exercising one on a transfer to a relative resulting from the borrower's death, or on a transfer where the borrower's children become owners, for a loan on fewer than five dwelling units. It limits acceleration; it does not decide who has to pay.U.S. Government Publishing Office
AmortisationThe way a fixed-rate loan is paid off: each level monthly payment covers that month's interest and retires part of the principal, so the balance falls to zero at the end of the term. The keep-and-borrow case on the estate liquidity page prices a fully amortising 30-year fixed loan at the published mortgage average, which over a long horizon costs more than the interest-only carrying cost shown beside it, because the principal is retired rather than rolled forward.Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (data: Freddie Mac)
Dwelling fire and landlord formsThe policy family a rental house or a building of up to four units is usually written on, as distinct from the owner-occupied homeowners multi-peril form. California Department of Insurance
Underpayment rateThe interest rate charged on tax that is paid after its due date: the federal short-term rate plus three percentage points, set every quarter by the IRS and compounded daily. It applies to unpaid estate tax from the original due date, whether or not an extension of time to file was granted.Internal Revenue Service

Extension of time to pay (section 6161)A discretionary extension, granted one year at a time for up to ten years, and only for the amount of the estate's actual cash shortage. It is not an extension of time to file, and interest runs from the original due date in any case.Internal Revenue Service

Section 6166 instalment electionA deferral of estate tax for an estate that is largely an interest in a closely held business: payment may be deferred for five years and then made in up to ten equal annual instalments, with interest at 2% on a limited portion and at 45% of the underpayment rate on the rest. How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
