Whether an array earns export credit, and how much, depends on the net-metering tariff it is on. Two things point the same way. The owner's $1,500 estimate is below the statewide average for an ordinary homeowners policy, and these are not ordinary homes: a $3,100,000 house in Cupertino and a three-unit pre-1979 building in San Francisco both require dwelling limits far above the average. On the other hand, neither property sits in a high fire-hazard severity zone — the Outer Sunset is a fog belt and Cupertino's flatlands are not wildland-urban interface — so neither should be pushed into the FAIR Plan, whose dwellings average roughly $2,918 a year.

The model uses the statewide average as a floor, which is a compromise: too high if a good admitted-market quote exists, too low if the dwelling limits require a specialty policy. This is the largest unresolved number in the cost model. Flagged item 4 →
