12 items could not be resolved to an official source. Not one of them is silently filled in: each is either labelled on the page where it appears or excluded from the figures. No item blocks the publication of this analysis, and none changes the recommended plan — but two of them (the San Francisco rate and insurance) could change the absolute numbers substantially.

How these are handled

A figure that cannot be verified is either (a) kept but explicitly labelled reported or assumption wherever it appears, or (b) left out and the row marked blank. No value has been inferred, interpolated or back-solved to make a table look complete.

What would close each item

  1. San Francisco rate — the parcel's actual secured tax bill for two consecutive years, showing the rate, the net taxable assessed value, and each direct charge as separate lines.
  2. Cupertino rate — the tax rate area printed on the bill, which maps to a single line in the county's rate book.
  3. Occupancy — the homeowners' exemption history on each parcel.
  4. Insurance — two written quotes per property, with the dwelling limit stated.
  5. Solar — the net-metering tariff name and the interconnection date on the utility account.
  6. Appreciation — not closable. It is a forecast; the range is published instead.
  7. Prop 19 allowance — the Board of Equalization letter to assessors issued each February of an odd year.
  8. Vacant-unit rent — two comparable rental listings in the same ZIP code.
  9. Future rates — not closable; the county rate book is published annually.
  10. County transfer tax — the Santa Clara County fee schedule and the city of Cupertino's current fee schedule at the recorder.
  11. Pending legislation — a search of the Legislature's bill database for estate and wealth tax measures in the current session.
