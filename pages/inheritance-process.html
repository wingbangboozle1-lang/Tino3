Inheritance process, step by step

Trust administration to recorded deed to assessor claim: the twenty steps, the four statutory deadlines, and what each one costs.

How to read this page

Twenty steps, in the order they happen. Steps 1–10 are common to every plan. Steps 11–14 are the ones that decide how much property tax you pay for as long as you own the houses. Steps 15–20 split by what you decide to do.

The single most important thing on this page: the assessor does not wait for you. Recording the deed starts a clock, and every relief available to you is claimed on a form that you must file. Nothing is automatic.

9Prepare and record the deedThe trustee executes a deed transferring title to the beneficiary (or to the continuing trust). The deed is recorded in the county where the property sits — Santa Clara County for Cupertino, San Francisco for the Outer Sunset building.
Recording is what tells the assessor a change in ownership has happened. It starts every clock below.		Before any sale or refinanceCounty recorderRecording $100–$300; SF adds $10 per deed subject to transfer tax
10Deed transfers inside a trust are not a saleA transfer from a trust to a beneficiary is generally exempt from documentary transfer tax, and is exempt from reassessment only to the extent Prop 19 allows. The exemption claim is made by affidavit at recording.
Do not assume the transfer tax exemption also means a property tax exemption. They are separate questions decided by separate offices.		At recordingS11 (transfer tax exemptions)$0
11File the change-of-ownership statementThe assessor mails a questionnaire after recording. Return it. Non-response lets the assessor reassess on the best information available, usually the sale price of a comparable.
Answering it is not optional in practice; a missed response is how people end up with an inflated base.		Within 45 days of the assessor's requestCounty assessor$0
12File BOE-19-P — the Prop 19 parent-child exclusion claimOnly if the property was the parents' principal residence and you will occupy it. The form claims the exclusion and is the single most valuable document in the file: on the Cupertino house it is worth $26,290 a year at 2045 values, or $677,830 over 21 years.
File it even if you think it will be denied. A denial is appealable; an unfiled claim simply does not exist.		Within 3 years for transfers on or after 16 Feb 2021, or before sale to a third party, whichever is earlierS01 S02$0
13File BOE-266 — the homeowners' exemptionThe $7,000 exemption (S16) is available only on an owner-occupied principal residence and only if claimed. It is worth about $90 a year; more importantly, granting it is how the assessor documents that you occupy the house, which is a condition of the Prop 19 exclusion.
The exemption is not automatic and does not transfer from the seller or the parent.		By February 15 to get the full year; within 1 year of transfer for Prop 19 purposesS16$0
14Move in within one yearIf you are claiming the exclusion, you must actually make the house your principal residence within one year of the transfer and claim the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption.
This is the requirement that most claims fail on. Changing your mind in year two undoes the exclusion prospectively.		Within 12 months of the transfer dateS01—

If the exclusion was applied at the wrong allowance period, the error compounds for as long as you own the house.		When receivedCounty assessor$0
16Appeal if the value is wrongAn assessment appeal must be filed with the county assessment appeals board between July 2 and December 31 for the following lien date, or within 60 days of a supplemental assessment notice.
The window is short and it is not extended for any reason.		Narrow statutory windowCounty assessment appeals board$30–$500 filing fee
17If selling: order the payoff and title workA sale within a few months of death is the cheapest possible exit, because the basis has just stepped up and the gain is close to zero (S18).
Selling before the BOE-19-P claim is filed is permitted, but it ends the exclusion claim.		At listingTitle companyTitle and escrow $3,000–$8,000
18If selling: pay the documentary transfer taxSanta Clara County charges $0.55 per $500 of consideration, plus city tax where one exists (Cupertino imposes none). San Francisco's rate is tiered on the entire consideration and is far higher: 0.75% from $1M to $5M, 2.25% from $5M to $10M (S11).
The tier applies to the whole price, not to the slice above the threshold. A sale at $5.1M costs three times as much transfer tax as a sale at $4.9M.		At recordingS11See the transfer-tax table on the taxes page

Rent-controlled tenancies cannot be reset to market while they continue; only vacancy decontrol resets them.		AnnuallyS13 S15—

The document file to build now

  • Certified death certificates — order 15.
  • The executed trust instrument, all amendments, and any schedule of assets.
  • Both property tax bills, most recent two years.
  • Both grant deeds, and any prior deeds showing the chain of title.
  • Homeowners' exemption records for whichever house is the parents' principal residence.
  • The solar interconnection agreement and net-metering tariff for the Cupertino house.
  • Both insurance policies, with the replacement-cost worksheets.
  • The two San Francisco leases, and the rent history for each tenancy.
  • Date-of-death brokerage and bank statements.
