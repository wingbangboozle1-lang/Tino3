A three-unit building in the Outer Sunset. Two units are let for a combined $7,150 a month; the third is vacant, which is what makes it possible to live there without displacing a tenant. Its assessed value is 6.3% of estimated market value — a Proposition 13 base set decades ago — which is why the current tax bill is so small.

One technical point on the homeowners' exemption, since it affects every plan in which you occupy the building: Revenue and Taxation Code §218(c)(2) treats a multiple-dwelling unit occupied by an owner as their principal residence as a qualifying “dwelling”, and a two-dwelling unit as two separate single-family dwellings (S16). The model therefore applies the full $7,000 exemption to the building when you occupy a unit. If the assessor instead prorates it to one of three units, the difference is about $60 a year — immaterial to any conclusion here.

Rent control, and why it matters to the decision

The two sitting tenancies are the reason this building is worth keeping. Under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance the annual allowable increase is set each year at 60% of the change in Bay Area CPI (S21), and for the year beginning 1 March 2026 it is 1.6% (S13). That is far below market rent growth, so the gap between the sitting rents and market widens every year — but the sitting rents are real cash that a house cannot produce.

Three structural points:

  • Rent increases are capped, but so is your ability to reset them. Rents reset to market only on vacancy, or under a Rent Board petition for capital improvement or operating-cost passthrough.
  • Owner move-in is a recognised ground for recovery of possession, which is what makes it possible to occupy a let unit. Relocation payments are payable to a displaced tenant — for notices served between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027 that is $8,245 per tenant, capped at $24,733 per unit, plus $5,497 where the household includes an elderly or disabled tenant or a minor child (S13). The owner must then occupy for a minimum period.
  • The vacant unit is the cheapest way to occupy. Using it costs the rent you forgo on one unit. Displacing a tenant costs up to $24,733 in relocation per unit, a legal process, and the rent on that unit anyway.

The rent-control mechanics above are structural, not priced into the model. The model lets the two sitting tenancies escalate at 1.6% a year and the third unit at 3.0%, which is itself an assumption.
