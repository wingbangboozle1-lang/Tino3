Decision in front of the family

Property A is expected to pass through a trust around 2040–2050; Property B is already owned by the heir and currently has two rented units plus one available unit. Do not treat the inherited home as already owned or assume Property B rent is displaced by living there.

Current model direction: sell Property A soon after inheritance unless the heir genuinely wants to live in it. This is conditional—not advice—and depends on eligibility, title, tax basis, actual carrying costs, market rent and updated law. A 2045 illustration puts inherited-home value around $6,531,230 and after-sale proceeds before reinvestment around $6,172,010; both are model outputs, not appraisals or quotes.

First questions for counsel and tax adviser

  1. Does the trust actually own the property, and is the transferor-to-beneficiary relationship eligible for California's parent-child Proposition 19 exclusion? If not, full reassessment is assumed. California Legislative Information California State Board of Equalization
  2. What are the trust's distribution, sale, occupancy, debt and trustee powers? Confirm deed, trust amendments, successor-trustee process and authority to sell.
  3. What is the date-of-death basis, appraisal standard and allocation between land/building? What is the cash estate, prior taxable gifts, marital portability and federal Form 706 filing position? Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service
  4. Which home will be the heir's principal residence after transfer, and can occupancy/claim deadlines be met? Confirm eligibility and filing sequence with the assessor. California State Board of Equalization

Mixed paths and the clawback

Living there for a period and then selling is the closest mixed path, and it still ends behind an immediate sale by $5,788,550 after five years. Moving out after claiming the Proposition 19 exclusion removes it: section 63.2(a)(1)(C) enrols the change-of-ownership base year value, costing about $244,980 of extra property tax over thirty years on the live-then-let path. Both readings are published; ask counsel which applies.

Known / unknown before choosing

Known from the supplied brief, not independently verified: owner-reported values, Property B rent and utilities, Property A water/solar estimate and historic tax-roll figures. Unknown: actual deed/title and trust terms, relationship eligibility, current insurance quotes, whether anything is registered against either parcel, property-specific utilities, repairs/capital work, exact tax-rate areas, Property A market rent, selling costs, total taxable estate and future law.

Two assumptions are now banded and tested instead of left as round numbers. Insurance: each building carries a band whose floor is the published statewide average for the matching policy form ($1,074 owner-occupied, $519 landlord on up to four units, newest year published 2017) and whose ceiling is a stated scenario; the whole band moves the thirty-year living figure by under 1% of the gap to selling, so a quote sizes the bill and does not decide the path. Encumbrance: nothing was reported either way, so both are modelled free and clear, and $1,000 a month of debt service costs a keeping path about $1,311,830 and the sale path nothing. Counsel's question is what is owed, not whether the transfer triggers repayment: on a building of fewer than five units, federal law does not let a lender accelerate because the property passed to a relative at death.

Obtain certified tax bills and assessor records, title report, trust copy, date-of-death appraisal, insurer quotes for owner-occupied and rental uses, utility bills, property-condition inspection and a local rent opinion. Do not publish source documents or identifiers.

Options and material risks

  • Live in Property A: possible reassessment shelter only if eligible and occupancy requirements are met; higher concentration and maintenance exposure.
  • Rent Property A: full reassessment may apply; income, vacancy, repairs, landlord rules and depreciation/recapture need a CPA-grade model.
  • Exchange instead of selling: at the inheritance there is nothing to defer, because the basis step-up removes the gain; the 45-day clock starts on the transfer, an exchange does not stop a reassessment, and it competes with the Proposition 19 exclusion for the same two years. Priced on the disposition page. U.S. Government Publishing Office Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service
  • Sell and reinvest: basis step-up may limit immediate gain, but confirm sale costs and tax. The published transfer tax and recording fees come to $5,520 on a $5,000,000 sale - about 2.0% of the modelled 5.5% selling cost - and the $75 instrument fee does not apply to a sale that pays the transfer tax; reinvestment risk remains. The Black Swan page models 24/36-month Treasury parking and phased equity entry through one canonical monthly path: a synthetic stress test and a dated backtest of the realized 1929, 2000 and 2008 returns. Reinvestment yield may decline at maturity.
  • How fast to sell, if selling: a sale in the same tax year as the inheritance pays no capital gains tax at all, and each year of waiting costs about a year of compounding, almost none of it tax. Ladder on the disposition page and lever 6 on the what-if page. Internal Revenue Service
  • Property B remains an independent owned asset: include its net operating costs, insurance, rent/legal rules and available unit in household liquidity planning.
  • If the taxable estate may exceed the federal exclusion: the tax is 40% of the excess and is due in cash 9 months after death. An extension of time to file is not an extension of time to pay, and interest runs from the due date at the federal short-term rate plus three points, compounded daily. Ask whether the section 6166 instalment election is available. Estate liquidity page. Internal Revenue Service U.S. Government Publishing Office (GovInfo)

Timing / action sequence

Before transfer: verify trust/deed, relationship eligibility, estate liquidity and insurance. At death/transfer: secure property, contact trustee/assessor/insurer, obtain date-of-death valuation, inventory expenses and confirm tax filing calendar. After title transfer: have counsel/CPA calendar assessor claim deadlines, income/estate returns, insurance endorsements and rental notices; model live/rent/sell choices with actual quotes before signing or distributing proceeds. Exact dates depend on transfer and filing facts.

Glossary

Basis step-up: generally resets inherited property tax basis to date-of-death value, subject to exceptions. Prop 19 exclusion: conditional California parent-child reassessment exclusion subject to statutory value and occupancy rules. Estate tax: federal tax on taxable estate, not a California inheritance tax. Reinvestment risk: future short-term Treasury proceeds may earn lower yields at maturity. Encumbrance: a mortgage, deed of trust or lien on a property; unknown for both buildings here, so both are modelled free and clear, with the limit on acceleration set by 12 U.S.C. section 1701j-3(d). Like-kind exchange: a section 1031 swap that defers gain rather than forgiving it, on a 45-day identification and 180-day completion clock.
