Decision in front of the family

Property A is expected to pass through a trust around 2040–2050; Property B is already owned by the heir, with two rented units and one available. Do not treat the inherited home as already owned, or assume Property B rent is displaced by living there.

Current model direction: sell Property A soon after inheritance unless the heir genuinely wants to live in it. Conditional, not advice, and dependent on eligibility, title, basis, carrying costs, market rent and updated law. A 2045 illustration: inherited-home value $6,531,230, after-sale proceeds before reinvestment $6,172,010. Model outputs, not appraisals.

First questions for counsel and tax adviser

  1. Does the trust own the property, and is the transferor-to-beneficiary relationship eligible for the parent-child Proposition 19 exclusion? If not, full reassessment is assumed. California Legislative Information California State Board of Equalization
  2. What are the trust's distribution, sale, occupancy, debt and trustee powers? Confirm the deed, trust amendments, successor-trustee process and authority to sell.
  3. What is the date-of-death basis and appraisal standard, and the land/building allocation? What is the cash estate, prior taxable gifts, marital portability and Form 706 position? Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service
  4. Which home is the heir's principal residence after transfer, and can the occupancy and claim deadlines be met? Confirm eligibility and filing sequence with the assessor. California State Board of Equalization

Options and material risks

  • Live in Property A: reassessment shelter only if eligible and the occupancy requirements are met; higher concentration and maintenance exposure.
  • Rent Property A: full reassessment may apply; income, vacancy, repairs, landlord rules and depreciation and recapture need a CPA-grade model.
  • Sell and reinvest: the basis step-up may limit immediate gain, but confirm sale costs and tax. Published transfer tax and recording fees come to $5,520 on a $5,000,000 sale, about 2.0% of the modelled 5.5% selling cost, and the $75 instrument fee does not apply to a sale paying the transfer tax. Reinvestment risk remains: the Black Swan page models 24 and 36-month Treasury parking and phased equity entry on one canonical monthly path, with a synthetic stress test and a dated backtest of 1929, 2000 and 2008. Reinvestment yield may decline at maturity.
  • Property B stays an independent owned asset: include its net operating costs, insurance, rent rules and the available unit in household liquidity planning.
  • If the taxable estate may exceed the federal exclusion: the tax is 40% of the excess and is due in cash 9 months after death. An extension to file is not an extension to pay; interest runs from the due date at the federal short-term rate plus three points, compounded daily. Ask whether the section 6166 instalment election is available. Estate liquidity page. Internal Revenue Service U.S. Government Publishing Office (GovInfo)

Known / unknown before choosing

Known from the supplied brief, not independently verified: owner-reported values, Property B rent and utilities, Property A water and solar estimates, the historic tax rolls. Unknown: deed and trust terms, relationship eligibility, insurance quotes, registered liens, property-specific utilities, repairs and capital work, tax-rate areas, Property A market rent, selling costs, the size of the taxable estate, future law.

Two assumptions are banded and tested rather than left as round numbers. Insurance: each building carries a band floored by the published statewide average for the matching policy form ($1,074 owner-occupied, $519 landlord, newest published year 2017); the whole band moves the thirty-year living figure by under 1% of the gap to selling, so a quote sizes the bill but does not choose the path. Encumbrance: nothing was reported either way, so both are modelled free and clear; $1,000 a month of debt service costs a keeping path about $1,311,830 and the sale path nothing. Counsel's question is what is owed, not whether the transfer triggers repayment: on a building of fewer than five units federal law does not let a lender accelerate because the property passed to a relative at death.

Obtain certified tax bills and assessor records, a title report, the trust, a date-of-death appraisal, insurer quotes for owner-occupied and rental use, utility bills, a property-condition inspection and a local rent opinion. Do not publish source documents or identifiers.

Mixed paths and the clawback

Living there for a period then selling is the closest mixed path, and still ends behind an immediate sale by $5,783,320 after five years. Moving out after claiming the Proposition 19 exclusion removes it: section 63.2(a)(1)(C) enrols the change-of-ownership base year value, costing about $244,980 of extra property tax over thirty years on the live-then-let path. Both readings are published; ask counsel which applies.

Timing / action sequence

Before transfer: verify trust and deed, relationship eligibility, estate liquidity, insurance. At death: secure the property, contact trustee, assessor and insurer, obtain the date-of-death valuation, inventory expenses, confirm the filing calendar. After transfer: have counsel and CPA calendar the assessor claim deadlines, income and estate returns, insurance endorsements and rental notices; model live, rent and sell with actual quotes before signing or distributing proceeds. Exact dates depend on the transfer and filing facts.

Glossary

Basis step-up: generally resets inherited property tax basis to the date-of-death value, subject to exceptions. Prop 19 exclusion: conditional California parent-child reassessment exclusion, subject to statutory value and occupancy rules. Estate tax: federal tax on the taxable estate, not a California inheritance tax. Reinvestment risk: short-term Treasury proceeds may earn less at maturity. Encumbrance: a mortgage, deed of trust or lien; unknown for both buildings, so both are modelled free and clear, with the limit on acceleration set by 12 U.S.C. section 1701j-3(d).
