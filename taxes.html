How much room is left for cash and investments

The exclusion covers everything in the estate: both homes, cash, securities and earlier taxable gifts. The amount of cash was not supplied, so the table shows the room left after the two homes. The 2026 and 2027 exclusions are the published amounts. From 2030 the exclusion is a scenario: $15,480,000 rising 2.5% a year. The last column cuts it in half to test a change of law. A negative number means estate tax would be due at 40% on that amount even with no other assets.

The table assumes both homes are in the estate, the conservative reading. The owner states (11 November 2026) that Property B is already owned by the beneficiary, so it is not part of the decedent's taxable estate IRS · Estate tax thresholds. If the deed confirms the statement, one person's room in 2045 is $17,612,290 instead of $13,819,960, larger by Property B's 2045 value of $3,792,330. The statement is owner-supplied; no deed was seen (flag FL-01).

What happens above the threshold. Each dollar of taxable estate above the exclusion costs the estate 40 cents. An estate $5,000,000 over the room would owe about $2,000,000 before credits. The tax is levied on the estate, not on the person who receives the property, and it is due with Form 706 nine months after the death; California and its cities add nothing. Because the exclusion is indexed and the room depends on house prices and on the cash balance, the comparison that matters in the year of death is the published exclusion against the date-of-death values, not today's numbers.
