Income tax on each path

Basis step-up, capital gains, the 121 exclusion, recapture.

The tax-free window

Property acquired from a decedent takes a basis equal to its value at death. Selling soon after the inheritance therefore produces almost no taxable gain: on the 2045 model the tax is $0 on a $6,531,230 sale. Hold the same house for thirty years and the tax becomes $4,910,950, even after the $250,000 exclusion. Let it out as well and depreciation recapture pushes it to $7,151,170.

That is the core of the recommendation. The window is not a loophole that might close; it is how the basis rules work, and it only exists at the moment of transfer.

What the model does and does not do

Not modelled, and flagged for a professional

  • The additional 1% California tax above $1,000,000 of income. Revenue and Taxation Code section 17043 adds 1% to taxable income above $1,000,000. A large gain in the year of a sale can push income over that line, which would raise the effective state rate used here. It is not modelled.
  • A like-kind exchange under section 1031. Exchanges of real property are still available, but they defer rather than forgive tax, and they do not fix the property tax reassessment. Whether one makes sense depends on whether the heir wants to stay in real estate at all.
  • An instalment sale. Spreads the gain, but not the tax.
  • Gifting during life rather than at death. Removes the basis step-up. Almost always worse for an appreciated asset.
  • Whether the trust itself should hold the property after the transfer. This affects both the basis and the Proposition 19 claim and depends on the trust terms, which this site does not have.
  • The federal deduction for state tax. Not taken. Taking it would slightly reduce the combined rate.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
