The headline

One decision matters more than all the others

Under Proposition 19 (S01) an inherited house keeps the parents' low property-tax base only if it was their principal residence and you move into it within one year. Even then, the protected amount is capped at the old base plus $1,044,586 (S02) — a cap the Cupertino house passed long ago.

Projected to 2045, the combined property tax on the two houses goes from $9,700 a year to $103,970 a year. That single change, not price appreciation, decides which plan wins.

Why the San Francisco building is the one to keep

Once both houses are reassessed to market value, the two properties behave very differently as investments. Cupertino becomes a house worth $6,531,230 that costs $85,500 a year in property tax and returns 0.5% a year net if rented. The San Francisco building, worth $3,792,330, costs $44,850 a year and returns 2.0% net, because two sitting tenancies are protected by San Francisco rent control (S13) and the building's Prop 13 base is still tiny.
