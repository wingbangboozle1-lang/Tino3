Executive summary

Bottom line: The estate-tax risk is low; the property-tax reassessment risk is high. Combined house value today is about $4,900,000, well below the 2026 federal estate-tax exemption of $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 per married couple), and California has no estate or inheritance tax. The real cost driver is Proposition 19: if the heir does not move into the Cupertino home as a primary residence within 1 year, the county reassesses it to full market value and the annual tax bill jumps from about $7,210 to about $41,390. Even with a move-in, the Prop 19 cap means the new assessed value is about $2,055,410 and tax about $27,450/yr. The San Francisco rental currently nets about $58,880/yr after modeled costs; a full reassessment would cut that to about $22,890/yr — so do not transfer the SF building as a rental without planning.

Recommended plan (see Decision page for full math):

  1. Keep the San Francisco building as-is (2 rented units + 1 owner unit). It is the cash-flow engine. Do not trigger a reassessment on it.
  2. Cupertino — conditional choice: If the family needs a primary residence, live in Cupertino (move-in within 1 year) to save ~$13,940/yr vs. renting it out. If no housing is needed, sell Cupertino promptly after inheritance (step-up basis makes pre-death gain tax-free) and invest the net ~$2,914,000. Renting Cupertino is the weakest option unless achievable rent exceeds ~$5,500/mo net of vacancy/management.
  3. Use a revocable living trust for both houses to avoid California probate (statutory fees alone ~$124,000 on a $4,900,000 estate, both sides).
  4. File Prop 19 forms on time (BOE-19-P within 3 years; homeowners' exemption within 1 year) if claiming the move-in exclusion.

This is planning information, not legal or tax advice. Confirm with a California estate attorney and CPA before acting. All projections are scenarios with stated assumptions, not guarantees.

Method & privacy

  • Anonymized: No names, addresses, APNs, or account numbers appear. Properties are identified only by city + ZIP. All personal details are placeholders.
  • Rounded: Every dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10.
  • Sourced: Every table row has a source link. Official sources (IRS, BOE, FTB, SF.gov, county assessors) are preferred. Secondary sources are labeled and flagged.
  • Assumptions labeled: Appreciation (3%/yr), insurance inflation (5%/yr), maintenance (0.5% Cupertino / 1% SF base case), market return (7% nominal) are ASSUMPTION, not facts.
  • No hallucinations: If a value cannot be confirmed, it is left flagged as unverified rather than filled in.
