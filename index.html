Recommendation in one paragraph

Sell Property A when you inherit it, and reinvest the net proceeds. On the base case (3.0% appreciation, 3.0% inflation, 4.88% risk-free reinvestment) selling produces $10,585,440 of after-tax wealth twenty years later, against $5,561,810 for living in the house and $8,134,340 for renting it. Selling also has the least moving parts: the step-up in basis at death means an immediate sale carries essentially no capital-gains tax, so the outcome depends on one verified interest rate instead of a 20-year rent forecast. Renting overtakes selling only if Cupertino values compound at about 5.4% a year or more and you can let it for at least $31,650/month (2045 dollars). Living in it is the weakest financial path and needs about 6.3% annual appreciation to break even — choose it only if living in Cupertino is worth roughly $251,180 a year to you.
