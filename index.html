What we can conclude now

  • California’s Prop 13 assessed value is not market value; annual assessed-value growth is generally capped at 2%, and reassessment may occur on change of ownership.
  • Prop 19’s parent-child exclusion is conditional: qualifying family home, transferee occupies it as a principal residence within one year, and timely homeowner-exemption filing. The benefit has a value cap; an investment/rental inheritance does not qualify as a family-home exclusion.
  • Federal 2026 estate-tax basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per decedent, but it is not a forecast for a death in 2040–2050. Federal estate tax applies to the taxable estate, not simply a cash inheritance receipt.
  • Using current unknown/unstated expenses, tax basis, and personalized circumstances, projecting net proceeds or recommending a sale would create false precision. Unverified values are left blank below.

Projection rule

Do not extrapolate the historic assessed-value series as the future tax bill. At inheritance, obtain the county assessor’s written determination and parcel tax rate. Prop 13 generally limits assessed-value inflation to no more than 2% annually after a base-year value, but change of ownership and Prop 19 eligibility can reset or adjust the value. Market-value forecasts, repair budgets, and future premiums remain blank unless supported by parcel-specific or qualified-source evidence.

04 · TRANSFER CHECKLIST

How the inheritance process typically unfolds

  1. Confirm ownership and governing documents. Trustee/executor identifies title, trust terms, beneficiaries, debts, and authority; get California estate counsel.
  2. Secure and document property. Maintain insurance, utilities, tenant obligations, mortgage/HOA records, and urgent repairs; do not alter leases without advice.
  3. Establish date-of-death values and basis. Commission qualified appraisals for each property and inventory financial assets; retain estate tax valuation and records.
  4. Review estate filings and liquidity. Executor reviews Form 706 filing requirement, portability election, gift history, debts and available cash. For 2026, IRS lists $15,000,000 basic exclusion; apply transfer-year law.
  5. File county assessor claims promptly. For potential Prop 19 family-home exclusion, check relationship and transferor/transferee occupancy requirements, one-year principal residence and homeowner-exemption filing deadlines; ask county assessor for written guidance.
  6. Get actual operating costs. Request binding insurance quotes for intended use, assessor tax estimate, utility statements, repair inspection, rental-market evidence, property-management quotes and sale-cost estimates.
  7. Compare after-tax scenarios. CPA/financial planner models live, rent, sell/reinvest including income tax, capital gains, property tax, operating costs, vacancy, investment risk and cash needs.
  8. Reassess before acting. Recheck law, trust, title, insurance availability and tax advice immediately before accepting, renting, moving into, or selling property.
