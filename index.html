Overview

Sell, rent or move in?

A costed answer for a home expected to be inherited through a trust between 2040 and 2050, with a second, rented property kept in view. Every rule links to its official publisher.

Best plan on these numbers: sell Property A soon after inheriting it, reinvest the proceeds, and keep Property B.

Inheriting in 2045 with house prices rising 4% a year, selling and holding a stock index fund ends ten years later with $10,052,910. Renting the home out ends with $8,583,500 and moving in ends with $7,945,420. All three are after every tax and selling cost.

Two rules drive this. First, the property tax resets when the home is inherited. Today's bill is about $7,340 a year. In 2045 it becomes $60,170 if a child moves in and $78,410 if the home is rented. Second, the sale is nearly tax-free, because the tax basis resets to the value at death.

Five facts that could change the answer

These are not known from the records supplied. Each one is on the Flagged for Review page.

  1. Who holds title to Property B today. The owner states the building is already owned by the beneficiary; the deed was not seen. Under that statement it is outside the estate and its tax does not reset. If the deed shows the parents instead, the tax resets as well: from about $3,790 to $46,600 a year in 2045.
  2. Which home is the parents' principal residence. Only that home can carry the Proposition 19 exclusion, and only if a child moves in within a year.
  3. Whether the trust is revocable. The tax-free sale depends on the home being part of the taxable estate at death.
  4. How much cash and securities are in the estate. Federal estate tax starts above the exclusion; under the owner's statement about Property B, only Property A and the cash use part of it.
  5. Insurance premiums. No official figure or quote could be confirmed. The ranking does not change at three times the owner's guess.
