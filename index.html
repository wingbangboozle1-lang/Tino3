Privacy and accuracy notice

  • What is published. Market values, assessed values, tax bills, rent and running costs, rounded to the nearest $10 as requested, plus the model outputs computed from them.
  • What is never published. No name, street address, parcel number, ZIP code, trust term, beneficiary detail or utility account appears on any page or in any published file. The two properties are called Property A (the home being inherited, in Santa Clara County) and Property B (the three-unit building already owned by the heir, in the City and County of San Francisco).
  • The inherited cash. The amount is not published. The estate page works entirely in anonymous scenario bands.
  • Rounding is not anonymity. Rounding to $10 defeats a search for an exact figure; it does not stop someone who already holds the county record from recognising a parcel. That residual risk is disclosed here rather than hidden.
  • Owner figures are unverified. Values taken from the owner's own records are labelled unverified unless an official public record was checked. They are listed again on the flagged for review page.
  • History. Earlier commits in this public repository, previous pull-request diffs, forks and external copies are separate exposure surfaces. Nothing on this site can certify them erased.

One fact is assumed, not verified, and everything else depends on it

The Proposition 19 exclusion applies only where the transfer is between a parent and a child, or between a grandparent and a grandchild whose parent has died. If that is not the relationship here, there is no exclusion: the property is reassessed to full market value, every shelter figure on this site disappears, and the case for selling gets stronger. The trust document is the only thing that settles it. Answer question A-1 before relying on any number here. California Legislative Information

The one-line answer

Under every baseline this site can build, selling Property A soon after it is inherited beats living in it or letting it out, and it is not close. The reason is a one-off combination: the inheritance resets the income-tax basis to the value at death, so an immediate sale pays almost no capital gains tax, while the property tax assessment jumps to market value under Proposition 19. Waiting converts a tax-free asset into a taxed one and adds roughly $68,490 a year of extra property tax if the home is let out.

The break-even house growth needed to make keeping the home beat selling is 7.72% a year for thirty years. The verified house price index for this area compounded at 6.9% a year over the last fifty years and 3.3% a year over the last twenty. Read the full decision, then the cases where this flips.

How to read this site

  1. Start with the executive summary and the decision page.
  2. Check the inputs. Every number the model uses is on the Property A, Property B and costs pages, with its source or an explicit unverified flag.
  3. Check the stress. No projection on this site relies on a single smooth growth rate. See black swan conditions and the verified historical record.
  4. Do not act yet. The questions page lists what is still unknown and how to find it out. The suggestions page is a review queue: nothing there has been implemented.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
