The decision

Five paths, priced, with the break-even numbers and the sensitivity.

What this site recommends

Sell soon after the inheritance. It leads in the baseline, in 9 of the 9 sensitivity combinations, and in 4 of the 6 stress regimes. It is also the only path that does not depend on the heir wanting to live in this particular house, being able to keep it as a principal residence, or finding a tenant at an implausible rent.

9 of 9Sensitivity combinations won by sellingThe rest are won by selling into Treasuries, which is still selling.
4 of 6Stress regimes won by sellingThe ones it loses repeat the 1970s or the 2021 to 2023 burst.

What the model leaves out

  • Housing value. Living in the house produces somewhere to live. The break-even table prices that value; the terminal-value table does not add it in.
  • Taxes on the invested money. The equity path already carries an annual drag. A further tax on final liquidation is not charged, because the same treatment would have to be applied to the property paths and would be avoided anyway if the portfolio is held until death.
  • State and federal interaction. The combined gain tax rate simply adds the two rates and takes no federal deduction for the state tax. That is deliberately conservative.
  • Mortgages, 1031 exchanges, instalment sales, gifting and trust restructuring. None are modelled. Each is on the questions page.
  • Transaction costs of moving house, and the cost of capital gains tax on any other asset that would have to be sold. Not modelled.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
