Reading the result. Selling and buying a stock index fund comes first in every case tested, and selling into Treasuries alone beats keeping the house in the base case. The margin nearly disappears in the least favourable combination tested: house prices rising 4.9% a year, stocks returning 5.0% and an heir who would otherwise rent a similar house. Living in Property A beats Treasuries when home prices rise near their 35-year average or a similar house rents for well above the placeholder. Renting Property A out never comes first. The gap between selling and living in the house is the price of choosing that home; that is a personal decision, not an error.
Timing. The house is reassessed from the date of death, so each month between death and sale costs roughly one twelfth of the yearly figures above. The one-year deadline to move in also runs from the date of death. Decide early.
