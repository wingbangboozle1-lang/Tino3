Decision analysis

Sell and reinvest comes out ahead

Three options, three inheritance years, three growth rates. All compared after tax, on the same footing.

Sell Property A after inheriting it and reinvest. Keep Property B and keep living in its third unit.

Selling leads in all nine combinations of inheritance year and house price growth tested below. Renting the home overtakes it only if the home appreciates about as fast as stocks return.

How the options are compared

Each option starts on the day Property A is inherited and ends ten years later. At the end everything is turned into cash and every tax is paid, so a house and a portfolio are measured the same way. Cash received or paid along the way earns the same after-tax return as the stock fund (4.96% a year in the base case).

  • Sell. The home is sold at its inherited value less 5.11% selling cost. Because the tax basis equals the value at death, no gain is taxed. The proceeds go into a stock index fund or into Treasuries.
  • Rent. The home is reassessed to full market value. Rent, less property tax, insurance, maintenance and vacancy, is taxed after depreciation. The family stays in Property B's third unit.
  • Move in. The Proposition 19 exclusion applies. The family pays the running costs and rents out Property B's third unit. Up to $500,000 of later gain is excluded on sale.

Why selling wins

  1. The sale is almost tax-free. The basis resets to the value at death, so the gain built up over the parents' lifetime is never taxed. Waiting ten years to sell turns later growth into a taxable gain at about 36%.
  2. The low property tax cannot be kept. Under current law the assessed value rises from about $802,030 to $4,984,990 if a child moves in, or $6,531,230 if the home is rented (2045). Tax follows.
  3. The rent is small against the value. First-year rent of $129,930 loses $78,410 to the reset property tax. After insurance, maintenance and income tax, $46,580 is left: under 1% of the home's value. The sale proceeds in Treasuries at 4.88% earn several times that.
  4. Concentration. Keeping the home leaves a large sum in two properties in one region. Selling spreads it.
