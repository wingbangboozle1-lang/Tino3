Property B · already owned

A separate framework for a property treated as already owned before Property A is inherited, including rental income and possible owner occupancy.

Ownership distinction. In this decision framework, Property B is already owned by the recipient; it is not treated as an asset newly inherited with A. Verify the deed, beneficial ownership and tax records before relying on that distinction.

Keep B's income and use separate

If B is rented, record collected rent, vacancy or non-payment, unit-level occupancy, operating costs, financing and taxes in its own ledger. If the recipient may occupy B, compare the housing value with rent that would stop or change, tenant/lease obligations and revised utility, insurance and maintenance costs. No rent or occupancy level is assumed here.

Separate tax ledgers

Do not combine B's existing rental receipts with A's estate value, and do not assume A's inherited basis applies to B. A tax professional should identify ownership, basis, prior depreciation, income allocation and the effect of any occupancy change from the underlying records.
