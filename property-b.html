Why Property B shapes the Property A decision

Three things follow from the heir already owning this building.

  1. The heir already has somewhere to live at no cash cost. That removes the usual argument for keeping an inherited house, which is that it provides housing. It also means the Opportunity cost of living in Property A is not rent saved but the rent the vacated unit here could earn.
  2. Property B is a going concern with a low assessed value. Nothing about inheriting Property A changes its assessment, because the heir already owns it.
  3. Two properties in one state means two reassessment clocks and one homeowners' exemption. Only one property can be a principal residence for exemption purposes, which matters directly for the Proposition 19 claim on Property A.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
