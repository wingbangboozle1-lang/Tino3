Black swan conditions

Regimes where the answer changes, and by how much.

Why a smooth 4% line is the wrong tool

Real economies do not grow at a constant rate. In the verified record used here, annual consumer price inflation ranged from minus 0.3% to plus 13.5%, and annual house price growth in Property A's area ranged from minus 12.3% to plus 30.2%. A single constant rate hides the thing that actually decides this case, which is whether the house and the stock market take their bad years at the same time.

Phased investment after a sale

What this test does and does not say

It tests the requested choice: after a hypothetical 2045 inheritance and sale, keep proceeds in short-dated Treasuries for 24 or 36 months, then move equal monthly portions into equities over the following 12 months. It compares that path with investing immediately.

The crash sequence is explicitly synthetic: an assumed -40% equity year, then +25%, then +10%, followed by the baseline 6.3% net nominal equity return. The 36-month wait therefore ends just as that invented three-year sequence ends; that coincidence is a scenario artifact, not evidence that a three-year wait avoids real crashes. It is not a reconstruction of 1929 or 2008 market returns. The Federal Reserve History links document the events and their timing, not these assumed percentages. The repository does not contain a source-verified S&P total-return series for those episodes; a true historical backtest is flagged as a data gap.

The treasury rate shown is a 4.7% long-run proxy derived from the Social Security Trustees' real-rate and inflation assumptions and historical 10-year yields. It is not a 3-month, 1-year or 2-year yield forecast. The actual bill/note yield, reinvestment rates, taxes on interest, transaction costs, and the chosen equity allocation must be refreshed with a fiduciary before implementation.

Interpretation and reinvestment risk

  • Waiting reduces the dollars exposed to an immediate hypothetical crash, but it does not eliminate market risk: equity purchases still face subsequent losses.
  • With no crash, waiting has an opportunity cost if equities outperform the Treasury proxy. The no-shock rows make that cost visible rather than showing only the favorable case.
  • Short Treasuries mature frequently. If market yields are lower at maturity, proceeds must be reinvested at lower rates; if rates rise, existing notes' market value can fall before maturity. Neither future yield nor ladder is known today.
  • All terminal values omit estate-level investment tax details, a personalized asset allocation, withdrawals, fees beyond the model's stated drag, and changes in future law. Ask the CPA and investment fiduciary to rerun with an actual sale estimate and current Treasury quotes.

What the stress cases say

Selling and investing wins in 5 of the 6 regimes. Keeping the house wins only in: Great Inflation repeat. Where house prices in this area grew at an exceptional pace, keeping the house can win; that is a bet on one neighbourhood outgrowing the whole market, and the historical record says that sometimes it does and mostly it does not.

Two findings survive every regime. First, the property tax bill after reassessment is large and completely insensitive to how the economy performs: assessed value rises at the 2% cap whether house prices boom or crash. Second, letting the property out from the start is the lowest-valued path in 4 of the 6 regimes, because it pays the full reassessment and the running costs, and then competes with a diversified portfolio on yield.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
