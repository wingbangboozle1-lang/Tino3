Black swan conditions

Regimes where the answer changes, and by how much.

Why a smooth 4% line is the wrong tool

Real economies do not grow at a constant rate. In the verified record used here, annual consumer price inflation ranged from minus 0.3% to plus 13.5%, and annual house price growth in Property A's area ranged from minus 12.3% to plus 30.2%. A single constant rate hides the thing that actually decides this case, which is whether the house and the stock market take their bad years at the same time.

Each row takes the realised annual changes in the house price index, the consumer price index and the 10-year Treasury yield for that real 30-year window and applies them to these two properties. No smoothing, no resampling, no invented numbers.

What the stress cases say

Selling and investing wins in four of the six regimes, including both hypothetical stress cases. Keeping the house wins in the two regimes where house prices in this area grew at an exceptional pace, which is exactly what happened in the late 1970s and in 2021 to 2023. That is a coherent result: this decision is a bet on whether one neighbourhood outgrows the whole market, and the historical record says that sometimes it does and mostly it does not.

Two findings survive every regime. First, the property tax bill after reassessment is large and completely insensitive to how the economy performs: assessed value rises at the 2% cap whether house prices boom or crash. Second, letting the property out is the weakest path in every regime except the two house-price booms, because it pays the full reassessment and then competes with a diversified portfolio on yield.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
