How the ledger works

There are 119 sources. The date in the last column is when the entry was last read on the publisher's own domain. The ledger was opened on 13 November 2026 and has since been re-read in stages, most recently on 17 November 2026, when the figures listed in the 17 November recheck table were read from the documents themselves rather than from any summary of them; the 16 November recheck table records the previous pass. The "supports" column says exactly what the source is cited for; the "limits" column says what it does not cover. Sources are cited only for what they actually state, and an official publisher whose name or address would identify one of the two properties is deliberately not linked here: it is recorded on the register instead.

What this ledger does not do

  • It does not reproduce the rate schedules, thresholds or forms. Those change; read the linked document.
  • It does not cover local ordinances and local measures affecting the two properties, whose official publishers are withheld because naming them would identify the buildings; those are recorded on the register with the date they were read. Nor does it verify which rules apply to which building.
  • It contains no paid, paywalled or secondary source. Everything here is an official publisher or an official government site.
  • It was checked on one date. Statutory sections are amended; letters to assessors are superseded; election results are certified after the fact.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.
