Pass of 12 November 2026 (merged from PR #53)

A later session re-opened four documents on the publishers' own domains and checked them against what this edition says. Proposition 19 on the Board of Equalization's page — the comparison chart and questions 2, 3 and 6 — confirms the mechanics the model uses: the family home is excluded only while at least one heir lives in it; the value limit is the prior taxable value plus $1,000,000, adjusted every second year; and a home that is rented does not qualify at all. The worked example in question 6 reaches the same taxable value as the formula in the model, with the $1,000,000 amount the model indexes each second year. The IRS estate page was re-opened: the 2026 filing threshold is $15,000,000 and the portability election is made on a timely filed return. That page does not state the 40% rate, so FL-16 stays open.

Two rules the brief asks about had never appeared in this edition. Both are now read at the publisher and published on the tax page: an inheritance is not income to the heir (26 U.S.C. 102, subsection (a), read on the Government Publishing Office's site, with subsection (b) leaving the income from the property taxable), and an inherited retirement account must generally be emptied within ten years where the owner died after 2019, with the exceptions the IRS lists. Two source badges on the suggestions page were also withdrawn: they pointed at the IRS estate page for a repository-history question and for a question about which edition to act on, which that page does not support. Those two suggestions are now listed without a badge. The legend's review window was extended to 13 November 2026. No model figure, projection row or ranking changed.

Additional check of the state gain rate, 12 November 2026

A separate session re-opened fifteen publisher pages the same day. Every already-published figure matched: the 2026 and 2027 exclusions, the Form 706 deadline and portability rule, the inherited-basis rule, the absence of a state death tax, the Proposition 19 conditions and the two-year amount, the local secured rate, the rent cap, the insurance survey's scope, the 10-year Treasury at 4.88% on 9 November 2026 and the index fund's 12.09% one-year return.

One gap was found. The one-time-gain rate used in the model is 20% federal, 3.8% net investment income tax and 12.3% California, and 12.3% is the top bracket of the graduated schedule. Section 17043 of the Revenue and Taxation Code adds a further 1% on the portion of taxable income above $1,000,000. It is published as FL-31 and C-14, with the dollar effect as a sensitivity rather than a model change, so no previously published figure moves: applied to the gain alone, the 2045 stock result falls from $10,052,910 to $10,016,750, and the sale remains first. Other income would push more of the gain above the threshold, so the figure published is a lower bound on that tax.

What this check did not do: it did not open the text of Proposition 19 or any local ordinance, contact an insurer, appraiser or broker, look up a parcel, or test the pages in a browser.

Repository review

Findings from reading the repository itself. These are observations, not sourced facts.

  • Published pages. Sixteen pages are generated from one model and one source ledger. A stale page fails the build.
  • Retired pages and data files. Paths used by earlier editions hold withdrawal notices with no figures. The build fails if any other tracked file appears.
  • Source ledger. Each source records how it was checked: opened, or read as an excerpt served from the official domain.
  • Checks. Every factual table row must link to an official publisher; a row that records a choice must link to the reasoning behind it. Dollar amounts must be multiples of $10. City names, postal codes, parcel numbers and e-mail addresses fail the build.
  • Publishing. The site is served from the main branch. Both publishing paths carry the same sanitised files. Earlier sessions report that the setting itself could not be changed with the available permissions.
  • Repository history. Earlier commits contain exact figures and location details. Not rewritten. Needs the owner's decision.
  • Pull requests from other sessions. Three pull requests (#23, #25 and #34) would each have replaced this edition with a model that predates the corrections listed above. They were closed without merging on 11 November 2026; their branches remain for reference. The substantive additions in #34 - a 40-window historical replay and an insurance framework - are covered by the two black-swan pages and by the sensitivity cases on the decision page, including insurance at three times the owner's guess.
