What this page is

Independent sessions re-checked the edition, line by line, against the official sources. Thirteen items were corrected across four passes; the last was found on 12 November 2026. The largest effects came from C-04, C-05 and C-06. The earlier edition's source work was sound: its quotations matched the official pages wherever they were re-opened.

What the 11 November 2026 pass added

Twelve sources in the ledger were opened or re-opened on the publisher's own domain on 11 November 2026: the IRS estate page and the 2027 inflation release, the Proposition 19 page and Letters 2025/009 and 2026/002, Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2, the local secured-tax page, the rent-increase page, the Treasury yield table, the inherited-basis FAQ, the HUD dataset documentation and the Federal Reserve inflation-goal FAQ. Every quoted figure matched what was already published; no number changed. Fifteen sources were added for the new black-swan conditions page (Federal Reserve, Federal Reserve History, BLS, CBO, SSA, the state election results service and six FRED series), the source library now holds 69 entries, and three stress cases were added to the model: stocks at 4.0% a year, inflation at 4.0% a year, and a flat decade for house prices. Selling still finishes first in all three. The law watch gained the unofficial 3 November 2026 ballot results and a pending local rent measure, and the flagged list gained one item: any debt on the properties.

Second pass, 11 November 2026

A third session re-checked this edition on 11 November 2026 before publishing it. Thirteen ledger entries were re-opened on the publishers' own domains: the IRS estate page, the 2026 Form 706 instructions, the 2027 inflation release, the Proposition 19 page and Letter 2025/009, Revenue and Taxation Code sections 63.2 and 13301, the local secured-tax page, the local rent-increase page, the Department of Insurance premium-survey page, the Secretary of State's initiative memorandum and its unofficial election results, and the Treasury yield table. The figures already published matched in every case; no number changed.

Three things were added or corrected. First, a gap: the state-tax treatment of Treasury interest had been left as an unverified assumption (FL-17). It is now read in 31 U.S.C. 3124 on the U.S. Government Publishing Office's own site, which exempts Treasury obligations and their interest from state and local tax but expressly not from an estate or inheritance tax; the flag is closed and the rule appears on the tax page. Second, the 40% estate-tax rate is now tied to the published 2026 credit of $5,945,800, which is exactly $345,800 plus 40% of the excess over $1,000,000; the rate table itself is still not opened, so FL-16 stays open. Third, a stale count on this page (58 sources) was replaced by a number the build computes.

The arithmetic was re-checked independently of the renderer: all 35 projection years for each property were recomputed from the model's own formulas (tax = rate x assessed value plus fixed charges; running costs summed; assessed value capped at 2%), the estate-room rows were recomputed from the exclusion less both homes, and the 2045 reassessment was recomputed from the Proposition 19 formula. Every published figure agreed to the nearest $10. The page structure was also changed: the menu is now grouped into five sections, the two black-swan pages are labelled as parts 1 and 2 of one section instead of appearing twice under different names, and the overview states which of the three editions in this repository a reader is looking at.

Third pass, 12 November 2026

A fourth session re-opened fifteen ledger entries on the publishers' own domains before this edition was published again. Every figure already published matched: the federal exclusion of $15,000,000 for 2026 and $15,480,000 for 2027 and the $5,945,800 basic credit; the nine-month Form 706 deadline and the portability rule; the inherited-basis rule; the absence of a California estate or inheritance tax and the constitutional ban on one; the Proposition 19 conditions, the $1,044,590 two-year amount (the letter states it to the dollar), the one-year homeowners' exemption and the three-year claim window; the 1.18162182% secured rate; the 1.6% rent cap; the Department of Insurance survey's scope; the 10-year Treasury at 4.88% on 9 November 2026; and the 12.09% one-year return on the index fund used as the official return reference.

One gap was found and is now published as a limitation. The one-time-gain rate used in the model was 20% federal, 3.8% net investment income tax and 12.3% California. Section 17043 of the Revenue and Taxation Code adds another 1% on the portion of taxable income above $1,000,000, which the 12.3% bracket rate does not include. It is recorded as FL-25 and SG-13, and the dollar effect is published as a sensitivity rather than folded into the model, so no previously published figure changes: applied to the gain alone it lowers the 2045 stock result from $10,052,910 to $10,016,750, and the sale stays first.

Two wording faults were corrected on the same pass. The flagged list had said it was ordered by how much each item could change the answer, although FL-24 sat between FL-15 and FL-16; the page now says the number is a stable identifier and groups items by subject. The source library and the review-date lines said 10 to 11 November; they now say 10 to 12 November, which is the span of the four passes.

What this pass did not do: it did not open the text of Proposition 19 or of any local ordinance, it did not contact an insurer, appraiser or broker, it did not look up a parcel, and it did not test the pages in a browser. It read the publishers' pages named in the ledger and recomputed the sensitivity from the model's own formulas.

Repository review

Findings from reading the repository itself. These are observations, not sourced facts.

  • Published pages. Fifteen pages are generated from one model and one source ledger. A stale page fails the build.
  • Retired pages and data files. Paths used by earlier editions hold withdrawal notices with no figures. The build fails if any other tracked file appears.
  • Source ledger. Each source records how it was checked: opened, or read as an excerpt served from the official domain.
  • Checks. Every table row must link to an official publisher. Dollar amounts must be multiples of $10. City names, ZIP codes, parcel numbers and e-mail addresses fail the build.
  • Publishing. The site is served from the main branch. Both publishing paths carry the same sanitised files. Earlier sessions report that the setting itself could not be changed with the available permissions.
  • Repository history. Earlier commits contain exact figures and location details. Not rewritten. Needs the owner's decision.
  • Pull requests from other sessions. Three pull requests (#23, #25 and #34) would each have replaced this edition with a model that predates the corrections listed above. They were closed without merging on 11 November 2026; their branches remain for reference. The substantive additions in #34 - a 40-window historical replay and an insurance framework - are covered by the two black-swan pages and by the sensitivity cases on the decision page, including insurance at three times the owner's guess.

How this edition relates to the cross-check

A separate cross-check edition was built by another session with its own model and its own source ledger. It leaves unverifiable inputs blank and reports break-evens. Both reach the same default: sell Property A in the first year and keep Property B. The cross-check compares selling with Treasuries only and finds that margin thin; this edition agrees (renting and Treasuries finish close together) and adds the stock index fund, which leads in every case tested at a 7% return. The cross-check's reconciliation table describes the edition that preceded this one; items it raised about the 2027 exclusion, the City B rate, the Treasury yield, rent growth and the failed initiative are adopted here.

What was not done

  • No address, parcel or owner lookup was made in any public record.
  • No insurer was contacted and no quote was obtained.
  • Pages were checked by build rules and by reading the generated files, not in a browser.
