Twelve sources in the ledger were opened or re-opened on the publisher's own domain on 11 November 2026: the IRS estate page and the 2027 inflation release, the Proposition 19 page and Letters 2025/009 and 2026/002, Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2, the local secured-tax page, the rent-increase page, the Treasury yield table, the inherited-basis FAQ, the HUD dataset documentation and the Federal Reserve inflation-goal FAQ. Fifteen sources were added for the new black-swan conditions page (Federal Reserve, Federal Reserve History, BLS, CBO, SSA, the state election results service and six FRED series), the source library now holds 70 entries, and three stress cases were added to the model: stocks at 4.0% a year, inflation at 4.0% a year, and a flat decade for house prices. Second pass, 11 November 2026

A third session re-checked this edition on 11 November 2026 before publishing it. Thirteen ledger entries were re-opened on the publishers' own domains: the IRS estate page, the 2026 Form 706 instructions, the 2027 inflation release, the Proposition 19 page and Letter 2025/009, Revenue and Taxation Code sections 63.2 and 13301, the local secured-tax page, the local rent-increase page, the Department of Insurance premium-survey page, the Secretary of State's initiative memorandum and its unofficial election results, and the Treasury yield table. The figures already published matched in every case; no number changed.

Three things were added or corrected. First, a gap: the state-tax treatment of Treasury interest had been left as an unverified assumption (FL-17). It is now read in 31 U.S.C. 3124 on the U.S. Government Publishing Office's own site, which exempts Treasury obligations and their interest from state and local tax but expressly not from an estate or inheritance tax; the flag is closed and the rule appears on the tax page. Second, the 40% estate-tax rate is now tied to the published 2026 credit of $5,945,800, which is exactly $345,800 plus 40% of the excess over $1,000,000; the rate table itself is still not opened, so FL-16 stays open. Third, a stale count on this page (58 sources) was replaced by a number the build computes.

The arithmetic was re-checked independently of the renderer: all 35 projection years for each property were recomputed from the model's own formulas (tax = rate x assessed value plus fixed charges; running costs summed; assessed value capped at 2%), the estate-room rows were recomputed from the exclusion less the real property the statement leaves in the estate, and the 2045 reassessment was recomputed from the Proposition 19 formula. Every published figure agreed to the nearest $10. The page structure was also changed: the menu is now grouped into five sections, the two black-swan pages are labelled as parts 1 and 2 of one section instead of appearing twice under different names, and the overview states which of the three editions in this repository a reader is looking at.

Repository review

Findings from reading the repository itself. These are observations, not sourced facts.

  • Published pages. Thirteen pages are generated from one model and one source ledger. A stale page fails the build.
  • Retired pages and data files. Paths used by earlier editions hold withdrawal notices with no figures. The build fails if any other tracked file appears.
  • Source ledger. Each source records how it was checked: opened, or read as an excerpt served from the official domain.
  • Checks. Every table row must link to an official publisher. Dollar amounts must be multiples of $10. City names, ZIP codes, parcel numbers and e-mail addresses fail the build.
  • Publishing. The site is served from the main branch. Both publishing paths carry the same sanitised files. Earlier sessions report that the setting itself could not be changed with the available permissions.
  • Repository history. Earlier commits contain exact figures and location details. Not rewritten. Needs the owner's decision.
  • Open pull requests from other sessions. Two open pull requests (#23 and #25) that would have replaced this edition with a pre-correction model were closed without merging on 11 November 2026; their branches remain for reference.

How this edition relates to the cross-check

A separate cross-check edition was built by another session with its own model and its own source ledger. It leaves unverifiable inputs blank and reports break-evens. Both reach the same default: sell Property A in the first year and keep Property B. The cross-check compares selling with Treasuries only and finds that margin thin; this edition agrees (renting and Treasuries finish close together) and adds the stock index fund, which leads in every case tested at a 7% return. The cross-check's reconciliation table describes the edition that preceded this one; items it raised about the 2027 exclusion, the City B rate, the Treasury yield, rent growth and the failed initiative are adopted here.
