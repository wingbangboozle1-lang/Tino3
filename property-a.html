Property A · inherited

A framework for an inherited property, including title, basis, possible California family-home reassessment treatment and blank evidence fields.

Scope. Property A is the inherited asset in this guide. No property description, address, assessment, market value, tax bill, occupancy history, cost or projection is published.

Rules to check

General rules relevant to inherited property
IssueGeneral rule summaryOfficial source
Federal basisBasis is generally the fair market value at the decedent's date of death. An alternate valuation date is available only under specified estate-return and election rules; taxable gain may apply on a later sale.Internal Revenue Service — Gifts and inheritances: inherited property basis and sale
California family-home exclusionA qualifying transfer must satisfy statutory relationship, property, occupancy, exemption and filing requirements. A transferee's principal-residence occupancy is generally required within one year for the family-home exclusion.California Legislative Information — Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
Filing periodsThe ordinary claim period is generally three years after transfer, before a third-party transfer, or when the eligible transferee stops occupying the residence, whichever is earlier; statutory and notice-related exceptions can change the deadline.California Legislative Information — Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
Change-of-ownership reportingCalifornia's statute sets different reporting steps for probate and non-probate transfers by death; obtain the current assessor form and calendar the applicable deadline.California Legislative Information — Revenue and Taxation Code section 480: change-in-ownership statements

Choices to compare

Keep for personal use, rent, sell, or defer a change while required administration is completed. Compare after-tax cash flow and housing value with the time, liquidity, insurance, maintenance and legal risks. See Compare options and Running costs.
