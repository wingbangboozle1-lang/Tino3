Four privacy disclosures

  • Repository history. Earlier commits in this public repository contain exact figures and location details, and earlier pull-request titles named jurisdictions. This edition cannot remove them. Rewriting the history or making the repository private is the owner's decision (suggestion S-12).
  • Jurisdictions. The pages name only County A and City B, but the official local links identify them to anyone who opens one. Dropping the local sources would remove that, at the cost of unsourced local rates.
  • Roll values at $10. The property tax and assessment tables now publish the county record rounded to the nearest $10, as instructed on 12 November 2026. The honest reading of that setting: it defeats a search for an exact number, and it does not defeat a search by someone who has the record. The growth chain from 2014 is itself a fingerprint. Tier 2 ($1,000) or tier 3 ($10,000) is materially stronger; the setting is a committed file, and the switch is one command (suggestions page, "the privacy choice").
  • What is deliberately not published. No name, street address, postal code, parcel number, utility account, employer, or the amount of the cash inheritance. People appear as [GRANTOR], [SUCCESSOR_TRUSTEE] and [BENEFICIARY]. The site has no forms, no scripts, no analytics and no remote assets, so nothing on it can collect anything from a reader.
Privacy. Properties are Property A (a single-family home in County A) and Property B (a small multi-unit building in City B). People are [GRANTOR], [SUCCESSOR_TRUSTEE] and [BENEFICIARY]. Assessment-roll values and tax bills are published at the precision the owner chose: roll values to the nearest $10. That is the owner's instruction of 12 November 2026. Rounding to $10 protects against a search for an exact figure, but a roll value that close to the assessed roll, together with a 2% growth chain going back to 2014, can still be matched to a parcel by someone who is looking. The stronger protection is tier 2 (nearest $1,000) or tier 3 (nearest $10,000); the privacy choice sets out the trade-off and how to change it. Every other figure is rounded to the nearest $10.
