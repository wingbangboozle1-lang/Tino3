Executive summary

The important details, the recommendation, and what would change it.

Recommendation

Sell Property A soon after it is inherited, unless the heir genuinely wants to live in that house.

Selling converts an asset earning nothing into capital, at almost no tax, and it removes a property tax bill that is about to multiply by six to eleven times. Living in the house is the only path that keeps the tax bill lower, and it still leaves the heir holding a single, undiversified, illiquid asset in one California school district. Letting it out is the weakest option in every scenario this site can build, because it combines the full reassessment with landlord costs and a rental yield far below the return available elsewhere.

The counter-case is real and it is on the black swan page: if house prices in this area repeat their 1977 to 1981 or 2021 to 2023 pace, keeping the house wins. That is a bet on one neighbourhood, not a plan.

Five things that could change the answer

  1. The heir wants to live there. That is worth the whole gap if it is true. It also has to be true for the whole time: moving out removes the Proposition 19 exclusion.
  2. Law changes. On unofficial results as of 12 November 2026, California voters approved Proposition 3, which makes the high-income tax rates permanent (60.7% yes), and rejected Proposition 40, the one-time billionaire tax (52.4% no). Certification is due by 11 December 2026. Both are on the law page. A future measure could cut the other way.
  3. The house outgrows everything. See the stress cases.
  4. The estate is large enough to matter federally. See the estate page: the 2026 exclusion is high, but it is indexed and can be cut by Congress.
  5. An input is wrong. Every owner-supplied figure is flagged. The questions page is the list of what to confirm and how.

How the numbers on this page are produced

Every computed figure on this page comes from scripts/model.py, which reads assets/data/inputs.json and assets/data/series.json. Monetary results are rounded to the nearest $10 before they are printed. The rules the model implements are the published official rules below; where a page uses a stated assumption or an unverified owner figure, that row says so and is logged on the register of unverified and assumed items.

  • California Legislative Information - Revenue and Taxation Code section 63.2
  • California State Board of Equalization - Publication 29, Property Taxpayers' Guide
  • Internal Revenue Service - Gifts and inheritances - inherited property basis and sale
  • Internal Revenue Service - Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses
