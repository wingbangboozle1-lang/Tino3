Quick summary

The public edition organizes the inherited-property decision without embedding a recommendation or any private property values.

Decision status: open. No option is ranked here because verified title, property-specific financial records, local law and the recipient's priorities are not part of this public edition. Use this summary to outline important details and the route through the full guide.

Five decision steps

  1. Verify title, transfer date, beneficiary designations and the controlling estate or trust documents.
  2. Calendar deadlines before comparing outcomes; some statutory periods are short and fact-dependent.
  3. Collect actual annual costs and one-time repair or transaction estimates. Keep them private.
  4. Compare A's use options separately from B's rental/occupancy choices, including the effect of lost or changed rent if B is occupied.
  5. Re-run the comparison under inflation shocks, falling asset prices, vacancy, insurance changes, major repairs and changes in law.

How a trust inheritance around 2040–2050 would be sequenced

  1. Confirm the trust terms, trustee and distribution provisions before the transfer; see trustee notice rule.
  2. At death, record the date-of-death value and request assessor guidance; see change-in-ownership reporting and family-home exclusion.
  3. Calendar the 60-day notice, 150-day assessor statement, one-year occupancy/exemption, three-year claim and nine-month estate-return periods; see Inheritance steps.
  4. Hold and then compare live, rent or sell using the private cost ledger and multiple economic paths rather than a single growth rate.
  5. Review Questions first and Suggestions for review before selecting any proposal or assumption.

Why a single 50-year growth rate is not used

Growth and inflation are uneven over long periods. The guide keeps a constant-rate illustration off the public pages and instead uses irregular historical episodes and a private irregular-path method. See Economic stress cases and Projection method.

Comparison logic

For a rental, start with collected rent minus vacancy/collection losses, operating costs, financing cash payments, major-repair reserves and applicable taxes. Keep taxable income separate from cash flow: a tax deduction is not the same as cash saved. For owner-occupancy, explicitly record housing value and any rental income that would stop or change.

For an inherited sale, obtain the basis evidence and sale costs; do not assume a sale is tax-free. See the Tax and law watch and Running costs.
