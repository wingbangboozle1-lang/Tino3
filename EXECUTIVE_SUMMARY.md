# Executive summary — inherited-property decision, 2040–2050

Prepared 10 November 2026. This document is the internal-use companion to the published site; it contains
no names, addresses, ZIP codes, parcel numbers or unrounded assessment figures. Properties appear as
`[PROPERTY_A]` (the single-family residence to inherit) and `[PROPERTY_B]` (the three-unit building already
held, currently producing rent).

> **Privacy.** Nothing in this file identifies a person or a parcel. Every dollar figure is rounded to the
> nearest $10. If you paste numbers into this project, keep names, addresses, ZIP codes and parcel numbers out.

## 1. The answer

**Sell `[PROPERTY_A]` within twelve months of the inheritance, keep `[PROPERTY_B]` rented, live in
`[PROPERTY_B]`'s vacant third unit, and reinvest the sale proceeds in a diversified portfolio.**

Renting `[PROPERTY_A]` out is the runner-up and wins under plausible conditions (details in §4). Living in
`[PROPERTY_A]` is the weakest financial option, because `[PROPERTY_B]` already supplies a place to live —
occupying A saves no housing cost, while giving up rent, a portfolio, or both.

## 2. Five numbers that decide it

| Measure | Amount | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|

| Same, if `[PROPERTY_A]` is rented instead | **$16,760,930** | Full reassessment + 33.3% tax on net rent |
| Same, if you live in `[PROPERTY_A]` | **$15,211,400** | No rent, no portfolio, full carrying costs |
| Return the sale proceeds must earn to beat renting | **6.5% a year** | Below this, holding wins |
| `[PROPERTY_A]` property tax in the first inheritance year, occupied | **$62,040** vs **$87,250** rented | Proposition 19 exclusion applies only while you occupy |

The sale is ahead by **$422,760** at 2045 — about 2.5% of terminal wealth. This is a narrow margin, not a
landslide, and §4 shows where it disappears.

## 3. Why the sale is the right default

1. **The basis reset is a one-time, date-stamped benefit.** Inherited property takes a basis equal to
   date-of-death value, so a prompt sale realises almost no gain. Every year of delay converts untaxed
   appreciation into a 32.3% gain at eventual sale.
2. **The Proposition 19 relief is capped and mostly exhausted by then.** The cap is the factored base year
   value plus $1,044,590 (transfers to 15 February 2027, indexed subsequently). By 2045 the projected value of
   $6,531,230 exceeds base year value plus cap by roughly $3,843,000, so the exclusion preserves only part of
   the assessment — and only while you keep living there.
3. **Renting it out is thin.** Full reassessment puts the tax at $87,250 in the first year against roughly
   $195,940 of gross rent; after vacancy, insurance, water, electricity, maintenance and income tax, the net
   is a small fraction of the capital at risk.
4. **Concentration.** One house in one county with one buyer pool, versus a diversified portfolio that can
   fund housing, care and expenses without a forced sale.
5. **Housing is already solved.** `[PROPERTY_B]`'s third unit is available. Living in A adds lifestyle, not
   housing.

## 4. What changes the answer

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Appreciation 5% a year, any reinvestment return tested (4–7%) | **Renting wins** by $760,000 to $1,870,000 |
| Appreciation 4% and reinvestment of 4–6% | **Renting wins** by $440,000 to $580,000 |
| Appreciation 4% and reinvestment of 7% | Selling wins by $422,760 |
| Appreciation 3% and reinvestment 6% or more | Selling wins |
| Proposal A.G. File 25-0017 enacted (restores full inherited-home exclusion from 1 Jan 2027) | Renting gains **$719,050** by 2055 and overtakes selling |
| Long-run portfolio returns 4% (bonds) | Selling is the **worst** option tested |

**The pending law change matters as much as the market.** LAO's October 2025 analysis describes initiative
25-0017 restoring pre-Proposition 19 rules — all inherited primary homes regardless of value, retroactive to
transfers since 16 February 2021. It did **not** appear on the 3 November 2026 statewide ballot
(California Secretary of State), so it is not law and not modelled in the base case. It is the single largest
swing factor in the analysis.

## 5. Inheritance tax: the direct answer

- **California: $0, at any amount.** Revenue & Taxation Code §13301 prohibits the state and every political
  subdivision from imposing any gift, inheritance, succession, legacy, income or estate tax on a transfer by
  reason of death.
- **Federal: a threshold on the estate, not on each beneficiary.** Form 706 is required when the gross estate
  plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds **$15,000,000** (2026). Below that, nothing is due; above it, tax applies
  only to the excess.
- **On your projected values**, the two properties total **$10,323,560** in 2045 against an indexed exclusion
  of **$23,979,750**, leaving roughly **$13,656,190** of cash and securities that could still pass before the
  federal threshold. That is the practical answer to "how much can be inherited before tax": at these
  valuations, about thirteen to fourteen million dollars of additional liquid assets, and no California tax
  at any level.

## 6. Step-by-step inheritance process

| When | Step | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Trust becomes irrevocable; successor trustee takes office | Trust instrument |
| Within 60 days | Serve notification by trustee on each beneficiary and heir | Probate Code §16061.7 |
| Within 60 days | Order a date-of-death appraisal for each property (fixes basis) | IRS inherited-basis rule |
| Within 150 days | File the change-in-ownership statement with the county assessor | Revenue & Taxation Code §480 |
| Within 1 year | File for the homeowners' exemption on the residence you will occupy | Proposition 19 requirements |
| Within 3 years | Claim the Proposition 19 exclusion (or before any sale to a third party) | Proposition 19 requirements |
| Within 9 months | File Form 706 if the estate exceeds $15,000,000; 6-month extension available | IRS filing threshold |
| Months 1–12 | Decide live / rent / sell — sell inside year one to keep the gain near zero | Basis and gain rules |
| At sale | Escrow pays transfer taxes; Form 1099-S and Schedule D filed even if gain is excluded | IRS home-sale reporting |
