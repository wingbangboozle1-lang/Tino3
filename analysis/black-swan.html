Black-Swan Economic Conditions & 50-Year Historical California Replay

Deep-think macroeconomic stress testing built directly from 50 years of official California FHFA house prices (1976–2026), BOE Consumer Price Index & Prop 13 factors, Federal Reserve 10-Year Treasury yields, and statutory Proposition 8 decline-in-value rules.

What Deep Thinking About 50-Year Real-World Regimes Reveals (That Constant-Rate Tables Miss):

1. A 100%-Equity Lump Sum Has Sequence-of-Returns Vulnerability, Solved by the 60/40 Treasury Barbell: If a 2008-style crash (−50% stocks, −35% housing) strikes right after inheriting in 2045 (BS-3 & BS-1B), 100% stocks drops to $6,026,950, losing slightly to renting [PROPERTY_A] ($6,129,520, aided by 9 years of Proposition 8 tax cuts). Yet selling into CA-tax-exempt U.S. Treasuries ($8,203,680) or a 60/40 Stock-Treasury Barbell ($7,068,910) beats keeping [PROPERTY_A] by $939,380 to $2,074,160!

2. Inheriting During a Housing Crash Flips the Optimal Decision (BS-4): If [GRANTOR] dies at the bottom of a −35% California housing bust, selling locks in a depressed $4,245,300 price, whereas holding [PROPERTY_A] permanently locks in a 35% lower Proposition 13 Base Year Value ahead of the recovery — making Keep & Rent ($7,466,770) beat Selling ($6,631,750) by $835,020.

3. Geographic & Seismic Concentration Risk (BS-5 & BS-7): Because [BENEFICIARY] already owns [PROPERTY_B] in the same metropolitan seismic zone, holding [PROPERTY_A] leaves 100% of household wealth exposed to a single tech-corridor downturn (BS-5: −$4,033,590 loss vs. Barbell) or uninsured earthquake deductible shock (CA Ins. Code § 10081, BS-7: −$2,157,960 loss vs. Barbell).
